🤖Robotics Basics

6 axes of freedom — meet the modern factory's most flexible worker.

Quick Definition

An industrial robot is a programmable, multi-axis manipulator — chained servo joints that position a tool (gripper, welder, dispenser) anywhere in 3D space.

4 Main Robot Types
🦾
6-Axis Articulated

Most flexible — welding, machine tending, packaging.

⚡
SCARA

Fast horizontal moves — assembly, pick-and-place on PCBs.

🕷️
Delta (Parallel)

Ultra-fast picking from conveyors — food & pharma.

🤝
Cobot

Collaborative — works safely next to humans, no cage.

Key Facts
  • ✓ DOF = Degrees of Freedom (6 = full 3D position + orientation)
  • ✓ Each axis = servo motor + gearbox + encoder
  • ✓ Programmed via teach pendant or offline simulation (RoboDK)
  • ✓ Repeatability typically ±0.02–0.1 mm
  • ✓ Top brands: FANUC, ABB, KUKA, Yaskawa, Universal Robots
Real Industrial Example
🚗

A car-body spot-welding cell uses 8 KUKA robots performing 400+ welds per body in 90 seconds — each robot following a path with ±0.05 mm repeatability, 24/7.

Quick Check

Q: You need a robot to pick chocolates flying down a conveyor at 200/min. Which type?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: A Delta (parallel) robot. Its lightweight arms move the gripper extremely fast, perfect for high-speed picking. SCARA is too slow vertically; a 6-axis robot is overkill and slower.
