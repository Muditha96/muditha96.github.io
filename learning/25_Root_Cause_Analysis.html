🔎Root Cause Analysis

Stop fixing symptoms — find the real cause.

Quick Definition

Root Cause Analysis (RCA) is a structured method to find the true source of a problem — so you can eliminate it instead of patching the symptom over and over.

Technique 1 — The 5 Whys
❌ Problem: Conveyor motor stopped working
1
Why did the motor stop?
The thermal overload tripped.
2
Why did the overload trip?
The motor drew excessive current.
3
Why did it draw excessive current?
The bearings seized — friction skyrocketed.
4
Why did the bearings seize?
They ran dry — no grease left in the housing.
5
Why did they run dry?
No lubrication task on the PM schedule — and no autonomous-maintenance step.
🎯 ROOT CAUSE: Missing lubrication task in PM schedule
Fix: Add to PM checklist + train operators (TPM Autonomous Pillar)
Technique 2 — Fishbone (Ishikawa) Diagram

The "6 Ms" framework — brainstorm causes in every category before deciding what to investigate.

Key Facts
  • ✓ 5 Whys: simple, fast, good for single-thread problems
  • ✓ Fishbone: broad, structured, good for complex defects
  • ✓ Use data, not opinions — confirm each "why" with evidence
  • ✓ Stop at human error → keep going to the system that allowed it
  • ✓ Pair with A3 / 8D for formal corrective action
Real Industrial Example
🏭

A CNC shop had 3 spindle failures in 6 months. 5 Whys traced it to: coolant filter never changed → coolant contamination → spindle bearing wear. Adding a filter-change task to the weekly PM eliminated the problem.

Quick Check

Q: A drill bit keeps breaking. Operator says "the new guy is using it wrong." Why is that a bad place to stop?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: That's blame, not root cause. Keep going: Why is he using it wrong? → No training procedure exists. Why? → No SOP was written. The system is the cause. Fix the system, not the worker.
