3S · SHINE (Seiso)

Clean the workplace daily. Cleaning is inspection — leaks, cracks, loose bolts get spotted immediately.

Japanese: Seiso — clean & inspect at the same time.
The 5th S — Sustain (Shitsuke)

The hardest step: make it stick. Daily audits, visual standards, training new hires, and leadership walking the floor — without Sustain, the area returns to chaos within weeks.

Key Facts
  • ✓ Origin: Toyota, Japan — 1960s production system
  • ✓ Shadow boards make missing tools instantly obvious
  • ✓ Red tags identify items to remove (Sort step)
  • ✓ Floor markings show paths, storage, hazard zones
  • ✓ Foundation for TPM, Lean, Kaizen, Six Sigma
Real Industrial Example
🔧

A maintenance shop rolls out 5S: shadow boards for tools, labelled spare-part bins, painted floor zones. Tool-search time drops from 18 min/day per technician to under 2 min — freeing 90 hours/month.

Quick Check

Q: A team has Sorted, Set, and Shined. Six months later the area is a mess again. Which S did they skip?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: Sustain (5S). Without daily audits, visual standards, and management accountability, every 5S effort decays back to chaos. Sustain is the hardest — it's culture, not cleaning.
