🌿Lean & 7 Wastes

Eliminate everything that doesn't add value — remember DOWNTIME.

Quick Definition

Lean Manufacturing systematically identifies and eliminates 8 wastes (originally 7 from Toyota) — everything customers won't pay for.

🔴 D · Defects

Scrap, rework, returns — any part that doesn't meet spec the first time. Quality cost = inspection + rework + warranty + reputation.

Example: Welding cell produces 3% rejects; root cause is robot torch offset drift between recipes.
The Shocking Truth — Most Time is Waste
Typical Manufacturing Operation
5%
95% Non-Value (Waste)
Only ~5% of a part's time in a typical factory is spent being transformed (the green slice).
The other 95% is waiting, moving, queueing, being inspected — pure waste. Lean targets that 95%.
Key Facts
  • ✓ Origin: Toyota Production System (TPS), Taiichi Ohno
  • ✓ Original 7 wastes were "TIMWOOD"; the 8th (Talent) was added later
  • ✓ Value = what the customer would pay for, knowingly
  • ✓ Tools: VSM (Value Stream Map), Kanban, Kaizen, Poka-Yoke
  • ✓ Lean works best paired with TPM + 5S + Six Sigma
Real Industrial Example
📦

A contract assembly plant maps a value stream and finds parts wait 11 days in WIP storage. Switching to Kanban + small-batch flow cuts lead time from 14 days → 2 days — same equipment.

Quick Check

Q: Building inventory ahead of forecast "just in case" — which DOWNTIME waste is this?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: O · Overproduction — the worst waste, because it causes the others: Inventory (storage), Transport (moving), Waiting (cash tied up), Defects (hidden longer before being caught).
