⚙️Bearings

The invisible workhorse — every spinning thing rides on them.

Quick Definition

A bearing is a mechanical element that allows rotation between two parts with minimum friction — by replacing sliding with rolling.

Anatomy of a Ball Bearing
Two Load Directions
Radial = perpendicular to shaft   ·   Axial (Thrust) = along the shaft
4 Common Bearing Types
⚪
Deep Groove Ball

Most common. Low friction, moderate radial + axial loads.

Load: Mostly radial
📏
Cylindrical Roller

Higher radial load capacity than balls. Allows axial drift.

Load: Heavy radial only
🔻
Tapered Roller

Carries combined radial + thrust loads. Used in pairs.

Load: Radial + Axial
⏫
Thrust Bearing

Carries only axial force — e.g. vertical shafts, gearboxes.

Load: Axial only
Key Facts
  • ✓ Replaces sliding friction (μ ≈ 0.2) with rolling (μ ≈ 0.001)
  • ✓ Standard bore codes (6204 = 20 mm bore, 6206 = 30 mm)
  • ✓ Lubrication is critical — grease or oil mist
  • ✓ L10 life: 90% survive predicted hours under rated load
  • ✓ Top brands: SKF, FAG, NTN, NSK, Timken
Real Industrial Example
🌀

An electric motor sits on two deep-groove ball bearings (one each end of the shaft). Replace them at the first sign of noise — undetected failure shears the shaft and destroys the rotor.

Quick Check

Q: A vertical drill spindle pushes downward against the workpiece. Which bearing carries the cutting force?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: A thrust bearing (or tapered roller pair). The cutting force is axial — along the spindle. Radial-only bearings would fail under the thrust load.
