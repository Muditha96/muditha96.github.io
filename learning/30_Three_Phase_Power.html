🔱Three-Phase Power

Three sine waves, 120° apart — the backbone of industry.

Quick Definition

Three-phase power uses three AC sine waves offset by 120°. This creates a rotating magnetic field — the reason industrial motors spin on their own without brushes or starters.

Live — The 3 Sine Waves
L1 · 0°
L2 · 120°
L3 · 240°
The Magic — Rotating Magnetic Field

As each phase rises & falls, the field rotates around the stator — pulling the rotor with it. No commutator needed.

Two Wiring Configurations

Y · Wye / Star

4-wire system (3 phases + neutral). Used in distribution. Phase = 230 V, Line = 400 V.

V_L = √3 × V_ph

Δ · Delta

3-wire system, no neutral. Used in heavy industry & motors. Phase = Line voltage.

V_L = V_ph
Key Facts
  • ✓ 3 phases × 120° offset = constant total power
  • ✓ Motors self-start — no starter needed
  • ✓ Power = √3 × V_L × I_L × cos φ
  • ✓ Star-Delta start reduces motor inrush by ~⅓
  • ✓ 50 Hz (Europe/Asia/Africa) or 60 Hz (Americas)
Real Industrial Example
🏭

A 50 kW factory compressor runs on 3-phase 400 V. Same power on single-phase would need 230 A wire — vs ~90 A per phase on 3-phase. Three-phase delivers more power on smaller cables.

Quick Check

Q: Why does a 3-phase motor start spinning without any brushes, capacitors, or starter?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: The three phases create a magnetic field that rotates at 50/60 Hz around the stator. The rotor sees a moving magnetic field and gets dragged along by induction. Single-phase motors need a starter capacitor to fake this rotating field.
