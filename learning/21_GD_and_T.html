📐GD&T

Geometric Dimensioning & Tolerancing — the language of drawings.

Quick Definition

GD&T is a symbolic system on engineering drawings that defines the geometry, position, and form of part features — far more precisely than plain ± tolerances.

▱ Flatness (0.05 mm)

The entire surface must lie between two parallel planes 0.05 mm apart — no datums needed.

Use when: a mating surface must seal evenly or seat on a flat gasket.
Key Facts
  • ✓ Standard: ASME Y14.5 (USA) / ISO 1101 (rest of world)
  • ✓ 5 categories: Form, Orientation, Location, Profile, Runout
  • ✓ Datums (A, B, C) establish the reference coordinate system
  • ✓ Modifiers Ⓜ (MMC), Ⓛ (LMC), Ⓢ (RFS) tune tolerance based on size
  • ✓ Replaces stack-up ambiguity → fewer drawings, fewer disputes
Real Industrial Example
🔩

An engine block drawing uses Position ⌖ on bolt holes referenced to Datums A-B-C — guaranteeing every cylinder head bolts up regardless of where exactly the holes are drilled (within tolerance).

Quick Check

Q: Why use GD&T instead of just ± tolerances on every dimension?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: GD&T controls function, not just size. Position ⌖ ⌀0.5 means "hole within a 0.5 mm dia. zone" — a circle, not a square. That gives 57% more allowable area than ±0.25 mm linear tolerances → cheaper to make, still works.
