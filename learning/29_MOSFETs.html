🔌MOSFETs

Voltage-controlled switches — the heart of modern power electronics.

Quick Definition

A MOSFET (Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor Field-Effect Transistor) is a voltage-controlled silicon switch — apply a small voltage to the gate, current flows from drain to source. No moving parts.

Key Facts
  • ✓ Pins: Gate (G), Drain (D), Source (S)
  • ✓ Gate draws nearly zero current → controlled by voltage
  • ✓ N-channel: gate +ve → ON. P-channel: gate −ve → ON
  • ✓ Very low Rds(on) — milliohms — minimal heat when fully ON
  • ✓ Drives motors, lights, heaters, switching supplies via PWM
Real Industrial Example
⚡

A VFD's inverter stage uses 6 IGBTs/MOSFETs switching at 4–16 kHz, PWM-chopping DC bus voltage into pseudo-sinusoidal 3-phase AC — letting one motor run at any speed with > 95% efficiency.

Quick Check

Q: Why does a MOSFET dissipate almost no heat at 100% duty cycle, but lots of heat at 50%?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: Fully ON → near-zero Rds(on) → low I²R loss. Switching between ON and OFF — during the transition the MOSFET is in its linear region, conducting current AT a voltage → big P=V×I loss. That's why switching speed (gate driver quality) matters so much.
