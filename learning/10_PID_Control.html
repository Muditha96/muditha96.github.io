🎚️PID Control

The control law behind every stable closed-loop system.

Quick Definition

PID is a control algorithm that calculates error (setpoint − actual) and combines three terms — Proportional, Integral, and Derivative — to drive the system to its target smoothly.

The Three Terms
P
Proportional
Reacts to current error. Bigger error → bigger push.
I
Integral
Removes steady-state error by summing past errors.
D
Derivative
Dampens fast changes — anticipates future error.

Key Facts
  • ✓ Used in temperature, pressure, flow, motion & level control
  • ✓ Tuning methods: Ziegler-Nichols, Cohen-Coon, auto-tune
  • ✓ Too much P → oscillation; too much I → overshoot
  • ✓ Output = Kp·e + Ki·∫e dt + Kd·(de/dt)
  • ✓ Built into every PLC, drive, and process controller
Real Industrial Example
🔥

A plastic injection mould heater uses PID to hold barrel temperature at 240 °C ± 1 °C — preventing burnt material on the hot side and unmelted pellets on the cold side.

Quick Check

Q: A heater is stuck 3 °C below setpoint forever. Which PID term fixes it?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: I (Integral). It accumulates the persistent error until the output rises enough to close the gap. P alone leaves a steady-state offset.
