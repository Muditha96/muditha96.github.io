🖨️FDM 3D Printing

Layer-by-layer construction — additive, not subtractive.

Quick Definition

FDM (Fused Deposition Modeling) melts plastic filament and deposits it through a nozzle, building a 3D part one layer at a time on a heated bed.

Key Facts
  • ✓ Common materials: PLA, PETG, ABS, Nylon, TPU, PC
  • ✓ Layer height typically 0.1–0.3 mm (smaller = smoother)
  • ✓ Anisotropic — weaker along the layer (Z) direction
  • ✓ Supports needed for overhangs > 45°
  • ✓ 7 process families: FDM, SLA, SLS, MJF, DMLS, Polyjet, Binder Jet
Real Industrial Example
🛠️

A machine tooling shop 3D prints custom end-of-arm jigs for robot grippers in hours instead of weeks — saving thousands per fixture versus machined aluminum.

Quick Check

Q: Why is a 3D-printed part often stronger when loaded across the layers (X-Y) than along Z?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: Layers are bonded by partial melting — that bond is weaker than the continuous extruded line. Force perpendicular to layers (Z axis) tries to pull layers apart at their weakest point: this is called anisotropy.
