A Chemical Spring You Can Wind 2,000 Times

Every lithium-ion cell is the same machine: lithium ions rocking between two host crystals, electrons forced to take the long way round.

Definition

A lithium-ion cell stores energy by moving Li+ ions between two intercalation electrodes — a graphite anode and a metal-oxide cathode — through an ion-conducting, electron-blocking electrolyte and separator. Because electrons cannot cross the separator, they must flow through the external circuit: that electron detour is your usable current.

On charge, ions leave the cathode lattice and tuck between graphite layers (LiC6); on discharge they "rock" back — hence the name rocking-chair battery. Nothing is plated or dissolved in normal operation, which is why a good cell survives thousands of cycles: the crystals breathe (~10 % volume swing for graphite) but stay intact. Degradation (page 8) is precisely the set of side reactions that break this clean picture.

The cell voltage is thermodynamics, not design choice: it is the difference in lithium chemical potential between the two hosts, set by the Gibbs free energy of the overall reaction.

E_cell = −ΔG / (n·F) F = 96,485 C/mol (Faraday) overall (NMC example): LiC6 + Ni-Mn-Co-O2 ⇌ C6 + Li(NMC)O2 graphite ≈ 0.1 V vs Li/Li+ · NMC ≈ 3.8 V vs Li/Li+ → cell ≈ 3.6–3.7 V
Inside the cell — the sandwich that does everything

One sandwich ~0.1 mm thick, wound or stacked dozens of times, is the whole cell. Every property — energy, power, life, safety — traces back to these five layers.

Nominal voltage

3.6 V

NMC/NCA cell. LFP sits at 3.2 V; a 12 V lead-acid needs 6 cells, an 800 V pack ~198 in series.

Cell energy density

250–300

Wh/kg for current NMC/NCA cylindrical cells; LFP ~160–210 Wh/kg but cheaper and safer.

Coulombic efficiency

>99.9 %

Charge out ÷ charge in per cycle. The missing 0.1 % is side reactions — that is your ageing (page 8).

The Chemistry Zoo

"Lithium-ion" is a family, not a chemistry. Cathode choice sets energy, cost, life and how the cell fails.

Why nickel, manganese, cobalt, iron?

Cathodes trade three things: energy (nickel-rich layered oxides hold the most lithium at the highest voltage), stability (iron-phosphate's olivine lattice locks its oxygen in covalent bonds) and cost (iron and manganese are cheap; cobalt and nickel are not).

Rule of thumb: more nickel = more energy but touchier thermally; more iron/manganese = safer and cheaper but heavier per kWh. Industry moved from NMC111 to NMC811 (80 % Ni) chasing range, while LFP took over standard-range cars once cell-to-pack construction (page 3) recovered its density penalty at pack level. On the anode side, blending 5–10 % silicon into graphite raises capacity (Si stores ~10× more Li per gram) at the price of a ~300 % particle volume swing that must be managed.

Thermal onset = approximate temperature where the cathode starts feeding a runaway (page 7). LFP's covalent P-O bond keeps oxygen locked in — the single biggest reason it is the "safe" chemistry.

NMC vs LFP — the trade at a glance

Same cell, different priorities: NMC buys range; LFP buys life, safety and cost. LMFP and Na-ion are attempts to bend these polygons toward each other.

Formats & Pack Construction

The same sandwich, rolled or stacked three different ways — then multiplied into a 400–800 V pack.

Three formats, one electrode sandwich

Roll it (cylindrical), flatten the roll or stack it in a can (prismatic), or stack it in foil (pouch). Format changes cooling paths, swelling control and how a vent behaves — not the chemistry.

Format trade-offs

Cell → module → pack, and why modules are disappearing

Cells in series add voltage; in parallel add capacity. A classic 400 V pack is 96s (96 × 3.7 ≈ 355 V nominal); an 800 V pack runs ~190–200s. Parallel groups (e.g. 96s4p) multiply Ah and let the pack survive one weak cell per group.

E_pack = N_series · N_parallel · V̄_cell · Q_cell 96s 4p · 3.7 V · 50 Ah → 355 V · 200 Ah ≈ 71 kWh

Cell-to-pack (CTP) deletes the module layer — cells bond straight into the enclosure — recovering 15–20 % volume, which is exactly how LFP packs caught up with NMC modules. Cell-to-chassis (CTC) goes further: the pack lid is the cabin floor and the cells carry body loads. The price is serviceability: a single bad cell now means glue, not bolts. The BMS (page 6) is what keeps 200 series cells behaving as one battery.

The Electrical Model & the OCV Curve

To a control engineer a cell is a voltage source with baggage: one resistor for the instant sag, RC pairs for the slow recovery.

Open-circuit voltage — the cell's fuel gauge (almost)

OCV vs SoC after a long rest. NMC's slope makes voltage a decent gauge; LFP's plateau (plus a small charge/discharge hysteresis) makes it nearly useless mid-range.

Equivalent circuit — Thevenin with a memory
V_t = OCV(SoC) − I·R0 − V_RC V_RC → I·R1·(1 − e^(−t/τ1)) typical 50 Ah EV cell: R0 ≈ 1 mΩ · τ1 ≈ 30 s · heat = I²·R0 per cell

This 1-RC Thevenin model is what runs inside real BMS firmware: cheap enough for a microcontroller (see A36), accurate to ~1–2 % when its parameters track temperature and age. Electrochemical (Newman/DFN) models are for the lab; EIS spectroscopy (page 9) is how R0, R1, C1 get measured.

Note what doubling R0 (a cold morning, or an aged pack) does to both sag and heat — same physics as the MOSFET conduction loss in A29.

SoC & SoH — Estimating What You Cannot Measure

There is no state-of-charge sensor. SoC is always an estimate, and the art is fusing two flawed witnesses.

The two witnesses

Coulomb counting integrates measured current — precise over minutes, but it drifts (sensor offset × time) and never knows where it started. OCV lookup reads the fuel gauge from voltage — absolute, but only valid at rest and nearly blind on LFP's plateau (page 4). A Kalman filter fuses both: predict with coulombs, correct with voltage.

SoC(t) = SoC(0) − (1/C_n) ∫ I dt (η ≈ 0.999 charge efficiency) drift: 0.1 % offset on a 1C sensor ≈ 2.4 % SoC error per day — must be corrected
The Kalman loop every EV runs

On LFP, the correction step only "bites" near the curve's ends — one reason LFP cars ask you to charge to 100 % weekly: it re-anchors the estimator at a known point.

SoH (state of health) is two numbers, not one: capacity fade (today's Ah ÷ nameplate Ah) and resistance growth (today's R0 ÷ fresh R0). A pack can hold 90 % capacity yet feel weak because R0 doubled — power SoH and energy SoH age on different clocks. SoP (state of power) is the BMS's live answer to "how many kW may I draw for the next 10 s without breaching voltage, current or temperature limits?" — it is what actually limits your overtake and your regen (see A38).

Balancing & the BMS Architecture

Two hundred cells in series are a chain gang: the weakest cell sets the pace, and the BMS is the foreman.

Why cells drift apart

Manufacturing spread (±1 % capacity), temperature gradients across the pack (hot cells self-discharge faster) and unequal ageing make series cells diverge. In series, the first cell to hit 4.2 V ends charging for everyone, and the first to hit the low cutoff ends the trip — an unbalanced pack behaves like its worst cell twice over.

usable pack capacity = min(cell capacities) − imbalance 1 % imbalance on 71 kWh ≈ 0.7 kWh ≈ 4–5 km of range, lost every trip
Passive vs active balancing

Nearly every production EV uses passive balancing — imbalance is small if cells are well matched. Active balancing earns its cost where cells are mismatched by design: second-life storage (page 9).

BMS architecture — the nervous system

Cell-supervision circuits (AFE chips) measure each cell to ±1–2 mV and report up an isolated daisy chain. The master runs the estimators and owns the contactors — and must fail safe (open) on any doubt.

Balancing only runs during the top of a charge session — a big imbalance can take many sessions to bleed off. This is why a deeply mismatched pack "heals" slowly, and why replacement cells must match SoC before installation.

Thermal Management & Runaway

A cell is happiest exactly where you are: 20–40 °C. Below, it plates lithium; above, chemistry starts feeding on itself.

Where the heat comes from
Q̇_cell = I²·R0 + I·T·(∂OCV/∂T) joule (always +) entropic (± small) 350 kW fast charge on a 400 V pack: I ≈ 875 A → 100 kW-class pack, tens of kW of heat

Joule heat scales with I² — the entire thermal problem of fast charging in one exponent. It is also the argument for 800 V packs: same power at half the current means one quarter of the heat (see A38 page 3). Cold is the other enemy: below ~0 °C the graphite cannot absorb Li fast enough and metallic lithium plates on its surface — capacity loss at best, a dendrite short at worst. Hence battery preconditioning: the car heats the pack on the way to the charger.

Cooling strategies
MethodCapabilityΔT across packUsed by
Passive air~0.5C loadspoore-bikes, early Leaf (no active cooling)
Forced air~1C5–10 °Chybrids, small packs
Liquid cold plate2–4C fast charge2–5 °Cnearly every modern EV

Uniformity matters as much as capacity: a 10 °C gradient means one end of the pack ages roughly twice as fast as the other (Arrhenius, page 8) — and unequal ageing becomes imbalance (page 6).

Thermal runaway — the chain reaction

Propagation defence is pack design, not luck: aerogel or mica barriers between cells, vent channels that route 600 °C gas away from neighbours and out of the enclosure, a pyro-fuse to cut current, and regulation (UN GTR 20 / China GB 38031) demanding the occupants get a 5-minute warning before any cabin hazard — new proposals push toward no-propagation designs.

Degradation — Why Batteries Age

Every cycle spends a little of the 0.1 % coulombic inefficiency. Where it goes determines whether the pack fades gracefully or hits a knee.

The four horsemen of capacity fade

SEI growth

The solid-electrolyte interphase keeps thickening, consuming Li inventory. Grows ~√t — fast when new, slowing forever. The dominant calendar-ageing term.

Lithium plating

Metallic Li deposits on graphite when charging cold or too fast. Irreversible loss; dendrites can pierce the separator. The reason fast-charge maps are temperature-gated.

Particle cracking

Repeated 10 % (graphite) to 300 % (silicon) volume swings crack particles, exposing fresh surface that grows fresh SEI — cycling-driven, worse at high DoD.

Transition-metal dissolution

Mn/Ni ions dissolve at high voltage and high temperature, migrate and poison the anode SEI. Why storing at 100 % SoC in summer is the worst thing you can do.

calendar fade ∝ √t · e^(−Ea/RT) — roughly doubles per +10 °C, worse at high SoC cycle fade ∝ throughput · f(DoD, C-rate, T)
The knee — fade is not linear forever

Fleet telemetry shows modern EV packs losing ~1.8 %/year on average — most packs will outlive the car. The knee exists, but well-managed packs are retired or repurposed before reaching it.

What the owner (and the fleet engineer) can control
  • 1Park in the middle. Storage stress is highest at 100 % SoC and high temperature — long-term parking at 30–60 % is the single cheapest life extension.
  • 2Fast charge warm, gently cold. DC fast charging below ~10 °C without preconditioning invites plating; the car's charge map protects you if you let it.
  • 3Shallow beats deep. Two 40 % cycles age the pack less than one 80 % cycle — plug in nightly, cap at 80 % unless the trip needs more (LFP: 100 % weekly to re-anchor SoC).
  • 4Heat is compound interest. Every sustained +10 °C roughly doubles calendar fade — shade, garage, and a working coolant loop pay for themselves.

Second life & recycling — the retirement plan

An EV pack retired at 70–80 % SoH is a poor traction battery but a fine stationary storage asset: grid buffering tolerates lower energy density, gentler C-rates and the active balancing that mismatched, aged cells need (page 6). After second life, recycling closes the loop three ways: pyrometallurgy (smelt; robust but loses lithium to slag), hydrometallurgy (leach and precipitate; recovers Li, Ni, Co at >90 %) and direct recycling (rejuvenate cathode crystals intact — highest value, hardest to industrialise). EU rules make recovery rates and recycled content mandatory, which is quietly redesigning cells for disassembly — glue versus bolts becomes a regulatory question, not just a service one.

Recap, Failure Modes & Quiz

The eight ideas worth keeping, the six ways packs die, and a self-test.

Eight ideas to keep
  • 1Rocking chair: ions shuttle between intact host crystals; voltage is thermodynamics (E = −ΔG/nF), and everything that breaks the clean shuttle is ageing.
  • 2Chemistry is a trade: nickel buys energy, iron-phosphate buys safety and cycles, LTO buys speed and lifetime, sodium buys cost.
  • 3Format ≠ chemistry: cylindrical/prismatic/pouch change cooling, swelling and venting; CTP/CTC trade serviceability for 15–20 % more pack energy.
  • 4The BMS sees V_t = OCV − I·R0 − V_RC: one resistor and an RC pair explain sag, recovery and heat.
  • 5SoC is an estimate: coulombs drift, voltage lies on LFP plateaus — Kalman fusion of both is the industry answer.
  • 6Series = chain gang: the weakest cell sets pack capacity; balancing (usually passive) keeps 200 cells honest.
  • 720–40 °C or consequences: cold plates lithium, heat doubles fade per +10 °C, and runaway is a four-stage chain you design against, not hope against.
  • 8Age before the knee: √t SEI + throughput wear is predictable; retire to second life before plating and dry-out bend the curve.
Six failure modes every engineer should know
🔥 Thermal runaway

Internal short, overcharge or external heat starts the SEI→separator→O2 cascade. Defence: chemistry choice (LFP), barriers, venting paths, pyro-fuse, 5-min occupant warning.

❄️ Lithium plating

Fast or cold charging deposits metal on graphite — permanent capacity loss, possible dendrite short weeks later. Defence: temperature-gated charge maps, preconditioning.

⚖️ Imbalance → over-discharge

A weak cell reaches 0 V while the pack still shows charge; copper dissolves, later shorting the cell. Defence: per-cell monitoring, balancing, conservative low cutoffs.

📈 Impedance growth

SEI thickening and contact loss double R0; the pack meets voltage limits early and feels gutless though capacity looks fine. Watch power-SoH, not just Ah.

🧭 Sensor / estimator drift

A biased current shunt or an unre-anchored LFP estimator walks SoC away from truth — sudden 10 % jumps or a car that "dies at 15 %". Defence: OCV re-anchoring, redundancy.

🔩 Mechanical abuse

Crush, penetration, or vibration-fatigued busbars short cells externally. Defence: crash structure, UN 38.3 vibration proof, HVIL interlocks and pyro disconnect (A38).

Quick Check 1

Why can't the separator conduct electrons, and why is that the whole point?

Tap to revealIf electrons could cross inside, the cell would discharge internally (a short). Blocking them forces electrons through the external circuit — that detour is the usable current. Ions still cross to keep charge balanced.
Quick Check 2

Your fleet's LFP vans show SoC jumping 8 % after sitting overnight. Broken BMS?

Tap to revealNo — working as designed. LFP's OCV is nearly flat mid-range, so the Kalman correction can barely act while driving. After a rest near the curve ends, the estimator re-anchors on OCV and snaps to the better estimate. Weekly 100 % charges reduce the jumps.
Quick Check 3

A 96s pack of 50 Ah cells has one parallel group at 47 Ah. What is the pack's usable capacity?

Tap to revealAbout 47 Ah. In series, the weakest group hits both charge and discharge limits first — the other 95 groups' extra 3 Ah is inaccessible. The chain gang rule: min(capacities), minus any residual imbalance.
Quick Check 4

Why does an 800 V architecture make 350 kW charging thermally feasible?

Tap to revealHeat is I²R. Same power at double voltage means half the current and one quarter of the joule heat in cells, busbars, connectors and cable — the difference between a liquid-cooled cable you can lift and one you cannot.
Quick Check 5

Charging at −5 °C without preconditioning: what happens and when do you find out?

Tap to revealGraphite can't intercalate Li fast enough, so metallic lithium plates on the anode. You find out later: permanent capacity loss immediately, and possibly a dendrite-induced internal short days or weeks afterwards. This delayed failure is why charge maps are temperature-gated.
Quick Check 6

Capacity SoH is 92 % but drivers complain the car is slow and regen is weak. Explain.

Tap to revealResistance SoH has degraded faster than capacity SoH. Doubled R0 means the pack hits voltage limits at half the current, so SoP (state of power) collapses for both discharge and regen. Energy and power age on different clocks — track both.
