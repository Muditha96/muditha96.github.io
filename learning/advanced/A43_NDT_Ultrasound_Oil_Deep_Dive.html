The Condition-Monitoring & NDT Toolbox

Vibration hears, thermography sees, oil remembers, ultrasound whispers — and NDT proves.

Definition

Non-destructive testing (NDT) evaluates the integrity of a material, part or system without impairing its future usefulness. Condition monitoring applies the same idea to running machines: measure symptoms, infer health, act before failure.

Two families share the toolbox. Machinery CM (vibration → A42, thermography → A41, oil, ultrasound) watches rotating equipment continuously or on routes. Structural NDT (UT, PT, MT, ET, RT) inspects static assets — welds, vessels, piping, castings — usually against an acceptance code. A senior engineer moves fluently between both.

The family tree
One decision tree: is the asset running or static, and is the suspected defect on the surface or in the volume? Those two questions select the method before any physics is discussed.
P-F

Earliest wins

Ultrasound & oil debris sit near vibration at the early end of the P-F curve — long planning windows.

5+1

Classic methods

VT (visual), PT, MT, UT, ET, RT — plus the CM technologies. Visual inspection is method zero, always first.

9712

People are certified

ISO 9712 / ASNT SNT-TC-1A qualify NDT personnel Level 1–3; ISO 18436 covers CM analysts.

Ultrasonic Physics — Waves, Impedance, Echoes

Everything ultrasonic — leak detectors and flaw detectors alike — runs on three equations.

Waves in metal

Ultrasound is elastic vibration above ~20 kHz. In solids it travels as longitudinal waves (particles push along travel — fastest), shear waves (particles move across — ~half speed, solids only), plus surface (Rayleigh) and plate (Lamb) modes.

λ = v / f wavelength sets detectable defect size (~λ/2 rule of thumb) v_long: steel 5900 · aluminium 6320 · water 1480 · air 343 [m/s] v_shear ≈ 0.55 × v_long (steel: 3230 m/s) 5 MHz in steel: λ = 5900/5×10⁶ ≈ 1.2 mm → resolves ~0.6 mm reflectors

Frequency is the resolution/penetration trade: higher f → shorter λ → smaller detectable flaws but more attenuation (scattering grows steeply with f). Coarse-grained castings are tested at 1–2.25 MHz; fine-grained wrought steel at 5–10 MHz.

Acoustic impedance — why echoes exist
Z = ρ·v [Rayl = kg/(m²s)] steel 46×10⁶ · water 1.5×10⁶ · air 415 reflection coefficient (energy, normal incidence): R = ((Z₂−Z₁)/(Z₂+Z₁))² transmission T = 1 − R steel→air : R ≈ 0.99996 → total reflection (cracks are air gaps!) steel→water: R ≈ 0.88 → 12% crosses — enough to work with

Two consequences run the whole discipline. Cracks reflect almost everything — an air-filled discontinuity is a near-perfect mirror, which is why UT finds them. And you cannot couple through air — the probe needs gel, water or oil (couplant) to smuggle sound into the part; a dry probe on steel transmits ~0.004%.

Pulse-echo and the A-scan
Pulse-echo: one probe sends and listens. Time locates the reflector (d = v·t/2 — divide by two, the sound travels there AND back); amplitude sizes it; a flaw echo before the back wall means something is inside the metal.

Airborne & Structure-Borne Ultrasound

Leaks, bearings, steam traps and arcing all shout at 40 kHz — you just need the translator.

How the instrument works

Turbulence, friction and electrical discharge all generate broadband ultrasound. The detector listens around 38–42 kHz and heterodynes (frequency-shifts) it down into the audible band — you literally hear the fault in headphones, with a dB(µV) level to log and trend.

Ultrasound at 40 kHz has an 8.6 mm wavelength in air: it is highly directional and attenuates fast, so the loudest point IS the source — unlike audible noise, which floods the room. That directionality is the tool's superpower: sweep, follow the loudest bearing, valve or fitting, and you have localised the fault.

The four plant applications
💨

Compressed air & gas leaks

Turbulent escape = broadband ultrasound. Survey under load, quantify per-leak cost (a 3 mm leak at 7 bar ≈ €3–5k/yr of compressor energy). The fastest-payback survey in industry.

⚙️

Bearing early warning

Friction & impacting raise the ultrasonic floor 8 dB (alert) → 16 dB (lubricate/watch) → >30 dB (failure) over the bearing's own baseline — the stage-1 detector before even the envelope spectrum (→ A42 p6).

♨️

Steam traps & valves

A blowing trap hisses continuously in ultrasound; a good inverted-bucket cycles. Internal valve passing is audible downstream when ΔP exists — no disassembly.

⚡

Electrical: corona · tracking · arcing

Each has a sound signature (steady frying / erratic buzzing / violent popping). Detects through cabinet seams without opening — pairs with IR (→ A41 p6) which needs line-of-sight.

Lube-by-ultrasound deserves its own line: grease while listening — friction dB drops as grease arrives, then rises again when over-greased (churning). It solves the classic "how many shots?" problem with feedback instead of a calendar guess, and over-greasing kills more small motors than under-greasing.

UT Thickness Gauging & Flaw Detection

The volumetric workhorse: corrosion mapping, weld inspection, and the modern phased-array upgrade.

Thickness gauging — corrosion's bookkeeper
d = v·t / 2 (t = round-trip time; the ÷2 is the #1 exam trap) corrosion rate = (d_prev − d_now)/Δt remaining life = (d_now − d_min)/rate

Calibrate on a step wedge of the same material (velocity varies alloy to alloy), zero the probe delay, and log thickness at fixed, labelled grid points (TMLs — thickness monitoring locations). Piping and vessel integrity programmes (API 570/510) are essentially disciplined trend charts of d over years. Hot surfaces need high-temperature couplant and delay-line probes; velocity itself drifts ~1%/100 °C — correct or your "corrosion" is thermometry.

Sizing rules of thumb: reflectors are compared against reference notches/side-drilled holes (DAC curves) or computed DGS diagrams; a defect echo crossing the DAC at the code-specified level is reportable. Sizing by amplitude alone is unreliable below ~λ — which is exactly why TOFD's time-based sizing became the crack-height standard.

Oil Analysis I — The Lubricant as Witness

Oil circulates everywhere, remembers everything. Sample it properly and it testifies.

Three questions, one sample

Every oil report answers: is the lubricant still fit for service? is the machine shedding wear metal? is contamination getting in? Fluid health, machine health, contamination — memorise the triad.

TestMeasuresFlag means
Viscosity @40/100 °CThe lubricant's #1 property (ISO VG grade)±10% from fresh: oxidation (up), shear/dilution (down)
TAN (acid number)Oxidation acids accumulatingRising TAN → oil ageing; abrupt rise → overheating event
TBN (base number, engines)Alkaline reserve neutralising combustion acidsFalling toward TAN → drain interval reached
FTIRInfrared fingerprint: oxidation, nitration, soot, additive bandsChemistry-level confirmation of degradation mode
Karl Fischer waterDissolved + free water in ppm>200–500 ppm in bearing oils: film damage, rust, additive hydrolysis
Particle countCleanliness → ISO 4406 codeRising code: ingress or wear — see next page

Water is the quiet killer: at 0.1% free water, rolling-bearing fatigue life can fall by more than half (hydrogen embrittlement at the contact). Demulsibility, breathers and seals are lubrication engineering, not housekeeping.

ISO 4406 cleanliness coding
count particles per mL at >4 µm / >6 µm / >14 µm each Range code = a DOUBLING band (code +1 ⇒ 2× the particles) example: 2500 · 640 · 80 per mL → ISO 18/16/13 typical targets: servo valves 15/13/10 · hydraulics 17/15/12 · gearboxes 19/17/14 (→ A13 Hydraulics)

Because codes are log₂, dropping two codes = 4× cleaner oil, and filtration upgrades show up fast. Cleanliness is the cheapest life-extension available: halving the 4406 level of a hydraulic system routinely doubles component life (Noria/fluid-power life-extension tables).

Sampling — where programmes are won or lost
  • 1
    Live zone, machine running, normal temperature — sample from turbulent flow (elbow upstream of filter, return line), never the drain plug sludge or the filter bowl.
  • 2
    Same point, same method, every time. Install sampling valves (minimess) — trend validity dies with wandering sample points, exactly like vibration points (A42 p3).
  • 3
    Flush the valve, use clean bottles, label with machine hours and oil hours — a perfect lab cannot rescue a dirty bottle.
  • 4
    Trend against the machine's own baseline; absolute limits are secondary. One sample is an opinion, five samples are a diagnosis.

The metal in the oil names the part that shed it — element by element, particle by particle.

Limitation that matters: spectroscopy sees particles below roughly 5–8 µm only. Severe fatigue spalls shed LARGE particles — a machine can be destroying itself while elemental ppm stays calm. That gap is what ferrography and PQ close.

Ferrography & particle shape
Morphology is the diagnosis: platelets = benign rubbing; chunky 3-D particles = fatigue; curls = abrasion by hard contaminant; spheres = rolling-contact distress. PQ index (bulk ferrous mass) trends the total iron regardless of size — the counterweight to spectroscopy's size blindness.
ppm↑

Rate, not level

A jump in Fe generation rate per 100 h matters more than any absolute ppm — normalise for oil hours and top-ups.

PQ

Large-particle guard

PQ rising while Fe ppm is flat = big fatigue particles: spectroscopy's blind spot, ferrography's cue.

Si→Fe

The ingestion chain

Dust in (Si) → abrasive wear (Fe up 2–4 weeks later). Fix breathers/seals, not just the oil.

Penetrant & Magnetic Particle Testing

Two surface methods, one division of labour: PT for anything non-porous, MT for ferromagnetic — and MT sees just under the skin.

Dye penetrant (PT) — capillarity as a detector
The five-step penetrant ritual. Sensitivity comes from the developer's bleed-out amplifying a sub-µm crack opening into a visible indication; fluorescent penetrant under UV-A in a darkened area is the high-sensitivity variant.

Failure modes of the method itself: smeared metal from grinding closes cracks (etch first if needed); over-washing empties them; too-short dwell finds nothing. PT is cheap and portable — and merciless about process discipline.

Magnetic particle (MT) — flux leakage made visible

Magnetise a ferromagnetic part; a crack cutting the flux lines forces the field to leak out of the surface, and iron particles (dry powder or fluorescent ink) pile up on the leak — drawing the crack in real time.

sensitivity rule: defect must CUT flux lines ⇒ field ⊥ crack finds it · field ∥ crack is blind ⇒ always magnetise in TWO directions (yoke turned 90°) detects: surface + slightly subsurface (~1–2 mm with DC) ferromagnetic only — no austenitic SS, no aluminium (use PT)

Field methods: AC yoke for welds (surface-concentrated field), prods/coils for bulk parts, bench machines with head-shot + coil for two-axis coverage. Demagnetise afterwards — residual magnetism attracts swarf into bearings and deflects welding arcs downstream. MT beats PT on speed and on paint-tolerant detection (thin coatings), and it sees the just-subsurface defects PT can never reach.

Eddy Current & Radiography

Induction for surfaces and tubes; photons for the full volume — with a safety culture all its own.

Eddy current testing (ET)

A coil carrying AC induces eddy currents in any conductor; a crack interrupts their path, changing the coil's impedance. No couplant, no contact, instant response — the inspection happens at the speed the probe moves.

standard depth of penetration (skin effect): δ = 1/√(π·f·µ·σ) currents at depth δ are 37% of surface value high f → shallow & sensitive · low f → deeper & coarser applications: heat-exchanger tube surveys (bobbin probes), aerospace fastener holes, weld caps through paint, conductivity/alloy sorting

ET's signature strength is tubing: a bobbin probe pulled through thousands of condenser tubes flags wall loss, pitting and cracks at metres per second, plotted on an impedance-plane display where phase angle ≈ defect depth. Limits: conductors only, depth limited by skin effect, and ferromagnetic tubes need magnetic saturation or remote-field probes.

Radiography (RT) — and its safety contract

X-rays or gamma (Ir-192, Se-75, Co-60) pass through the part onto film or a digital detector; density differences reveal volume — porosity, slag, lack of fusion, corrosion under insulation.

attenuation: I = I₀·e^(−µx) contrast comes from Δ(µx) image quality proven by IQI/penetrameter wire visibility inverse-square law: dose rate ∝ 1/r² → distance is the cheapest shielding

RT sees volumetric defects PT/MT miss and leaves a permanent image — but planar cracks aligned across the beam can vanish (orientation sensitivity, the mirror of MT's rule). Modern practice replaces much film with digital radiography and much RT altogether with PAUT/TOFD — no exclusion zones, instant results. Where gamma is used: controlled areas, dosimeters, collimators, and a licensed crew are non-negotiable — treat every source as live until proven stored.

    Certify the humans: ISO 18436 (CM: vibration -2, lubricants -4/-5, thermography -7, ultrasound -8) and ISO 9712 / SNT-TC-1A Levels 1–3 for NDT. Reports signed at the right level are contractual currency.
  • 2

  • 3

  • 4

Recap, Failure Modes & Quiz

Eight ideas, six ways the inspection itself fails, six checks.

Eight ideas that carry the module
  • 1
    Running vs static, surface vs volume — two questions select the method.
  • 2
    R = ((Z₂−Z₁)/(Z₂+Z₁))² — cracks mirror sound; air blocks it; couplant exists because of this one equation.
  • 3
    d = v·t/2 — pulse-echo divides by two; calibrate velocity per material.
  • 4
    40 kHz airborne ultrasound is directional — the loudest point is the leak, the dry bearing, the arcing joint.
  • 5
    Oil answers three questions: fluid health (viscosity/TAN), machine health (wear metals), contamination (Si, water, 4406).
  • 6
    ISO 4406 codes are log₂ — one code = 2×; cleanliness is the cheapest life extension.
  • 7
    MT/RT are orientation-sensitive; PT needs an open crack; ET obeys the skin effect — every method has a blind axis. Know it.
  • 8
    Two technologies agreeing = work order — the programme is a matrix with certified people and coded acceptance criteria.
Six ways the inspection lies
⚠️ Dry / cocked probe

No couplant or a rocked probe drops the back-wall echo — thin readings, phantom "corrosion". Watch echo quality, not just numbers.

⚠️ Wrong velocity

Steel-calibrated gauge on stainless or alu misreads every thickness by the velocity ratio. Calibrate per material, correct for temperature.

⚠️ Doubling error

A thin, corroded back wall can trigger on the 2nd multiple — reading DOUBLE the true thickness on the worst spot in the plant. Verify on the A-scan.

⚠️ Smeared or washed cracks

Grinding smears metal over cracks before PT; over-washing empties them. Process discipline IS the sensitivity.

⚠️ Single-direction MT

Field parallel to the crack = invisible defect. Two magnetisation directions or it did not happen.

⚠️ Bottle-sample oil

Drain-plug sludge or wandering sample points destroy the trend — the lab faithfully analyses the wrong oil.

🧠 Quick Check 1

Why can a hairline crack invisible to X-ray be a near-perfect ultrasonic reflector?

Tap to reveal RT needs a density-path difference — a tight crack across the beam removes almost no material, so contrast ≈ 0. UT sees the impedance interface: steel→air reflects ~99.996% of energy regardless of gap width. Orientation completes the story: cracks normal to the X-ray beam vanish; cracks normal to the sound beam blaze.
🧠 Quick Check 2

A UT gauge shows 6.78 µs round trip in steel (5900 m/s). Wall thickness?

Tap to reveal d = v·t/2 = 5900 × 6.78×10⁻⁶ / 2 = 20.0 mm. Forgetting the ÷2 gives 40 mm — the classic error that hides half your corrosion allowance.
🧠 Quick Check 3

Oil report: Fe rising, Si rising, Cu flat, PQ climbing, viscosity normal. Story?

Tap to reveal Dirt ingestion driving abrasive wear: Si (silica) entered first, ferrous wear followed, and rising PQ says particles include large ones. Fluid itself is healthy — so fix the ingress (breather, seals), flush/filter, and re-baseline. An oil change alone treats the symptom.
🧠 Quick Check 4

Why must MT magnetise in two directions, and what is the matching rule in RT?

Tap to reveal MT only detects defects that cut flux lines — a crack parallel to the field leaks nothing, so the yoke is rotated 90° and the area re-tested. RT's mirror rule: a planar crack must be roughly aligned with the beam to image; across the beam it disappears. Every method has a blind orientation — procedures exist to rotate around it.
🧠 Quick Check 5

Bearing point reads +16 dB over its ultrasonic baseline; vibration velocity spectrum is still clean. Action?

Tap to reveal +16 dB ≈ lubrication-needed / early-stage threshold: grease while listening (stop when dB drops), log, and re-check within days. If the level will not come down with lubrication, treat as stage-1→2 bearing damage: pull an envelope spectrum (A42 p7) to hunt for BPFO/BPFI and start trending. Clean velocity spectrum is expected this early — that is exactly why ultrasound leads the P-F curve.
🧠 Quick Check 6

Hydraulic oil counts: 5000 /mL >4 µm, 1200 /mL >6 µm, 150 /mL >14 µm. Assign the ISO 4406 code (ranges double per code; 1300–2500 = 18).

Tap to reveal 5000 falls in 2500–5000 → 19; 1200 falls in 640–1300 → 17; 150 falls in 80–160 → 14. Code 19/17/14 — two codes above a 17/15/12 target, i.e. ~4× too dirty: upgrade filtration and hunt ingress before it becomes valve wear.

Worked Examples

Four exam-grade numericals, solved step by step.

Worked Example 1 — Locating a flaw in a 20 mm wall

Given: pulse-echo on steel (v = 5900 m/s). Back-wall echo at 6.78 µs; an intermediate echo at 4.20 µs. Find wall thickness, flaw depth, and remaining ligament.

wall: d = v·t/2 = 5900 × 6.78×10⁻⁶ / 2 = 20.0 mm flaw: d_f = 5900 × 4.20×10⁻⁶ / 2 = 12.4 mm below the surface ligament to back wall = 20.0 − 12.4 = 7.6 mm

Discussion: the A-scan tells position for free; what it does not tell honestly is size. The flaw echo's amplitude vs the DAC curve classifies it for the code, but a tight crack tilted away from the beam can return a whisper — which is why critical welds get angle-beam coverage from both sides plus TOFD for height sizing, not a single normal-beam pass.

Worked Example 2 — Impedance chain: why immersion testing still works

Given: Z_steel = 46.3, Z_water = 1.48 (×10⁶ Rayl). Compute R at the water/steel interface and the round-trip transmission for an immersion inspection (sound must cross twice).

R = ((46.3 − 1.48)/(46.3 + 1.48))² = (44.82/47.78)² = 0.880 T = 1 − R = 0.120 per crossing round trip: T² = 0.120² = 0.0144 ≈ 1.4% of energy in dB (energy): 10·log₁₀(0.0144) ≈ −18.4 dB

Discussion: only ~1.4% of the energy survives entering AND leaving the part — and immersion UT works superbly anyway, because receivers have >100 dB of dynamic range. The same arithmetic with air (Z = 0.000415) gives T ≈ 4×10⁻⁵ per crossing, ~10⁻⁹ round-trip: unmeasurable. One equation separates "needs gel" from "physically hopeless".

Worked Example 3 — ISO 4406 coding and a filtration decision

Given: hydraulic oil counts 2500 / 640 / 80 particles per mL at >4/>6/>14 µm. Target for the servo-valve system is 15/13/10. Code the oil and size the gap.

code bands double per step (…, 13: 40–80, 14: 80–160, 15: 160–320, 16: 320–640, 17: 640–1300, 18: 1300–2500 …) 2500 → code 18 · 640 → code 16 · 80 → code 13 ⇒ ISO 18/16/13 gap to 15/13/10 = 3 codes at every size = 2³ = 8× too many particles

Discussion: three codes is not a top-up problem — it is an architecture problem: add kidney-loop filtration (β₅ ≥ 200 element), fix breather and cylinder-rod ingress, then re-sample after 50 h of circulation. Component-life tables reward the effort: moving 18/16/13 → 15/13/10 typically better than doubles servo-valve and pump life — the cheapest "overhaul" in the plant (→ A13 Hydraulics).

Worked Example 4 — Eddy-current skin depth

Given: aluminium (σ = 3.5×10⁷ S/m, µ ≈ µ₀ = 4π×10⁻⁷ H/m) inspected at f = 100 kHz. Find the standard depth of penetration and comment on frequency choice.

δ = 1/√(π·f·µ·σ) = 1/√(π × 10⁵ × 4π×10⁻⁷ × 3.5×10⁷) = 1/√(1.382×10⁷) = 1/3718 ≈ 0.27 mm at 10 kHz: δ ≈ 0.85 mm (δ ∝ 1/√f)

Discussion: at 100 kHz the test is a surface microscope — ideal for fatigue cracks at fastener holes; wall-loss surveys in tubing drop to tens of kHz to reach the far wall. Steel adds µ_r in the hundreds, collapsing δ to hundredths of a millimetre — hence magnetic saturation or remote-field techniques for ferromagnetic tubes. Frequency is the ET analyst's focus knob.

Problem Set — 10 Questions

Work each one before revealing. The physics pages carry every answer.

Problem 1

Aluminium (6320 m/s), round-trip 9.5 µs. Thickness?

Tap to reveal d = 6320 × 9.5×10⁻⁶/2 = 30.0 mm.
Problem 2

Wavelength and smallest-detectable-reflector estimate at 2.25 MHz in steel?

Tap to reveal λ = 5900/2.25×10⁶ = 2.62 mm; λ/2 rule → reflectors down to ≈ 1.3 mm. Choosing 5 MHz halves both — at the price of attenuation in coarse structures.
Problem 3

Compute R for steel (46.3) to perspex wedge (3.22 ×10⁶ Rayl). Why do angle probes still work well?

Tap to reveal R = ((46.3−3.22)/(46.3+3.22))² = (43.08/49.52)² ≈ 0.76 — only ~24% crosses. It works because probes drive plenty of energy, receivers are sensitive, and the wedge geometry converts the transmitted fraction into a well-defined refracted shear beam (Snell's law) — efficiency is traded for beam control.
Problem 4

Why does a UT probe read nothing on bare dry steel, and what fixes it?

Tap to reveal A microscopic air film separates probe and part: steel/air R ≈ 0.99996, so ~0.004% of energy crosses each way — nothing measurable returns. Couplant (gel/oil/water) replaces the air with a medium of workable impedance; on hot lines, high-temperature couplant or delay-line probes do the same job.
Problem 5

A filtration upgrade moves a system from ISO 19/17/14 to 17/15/12. By what factor did particle counts fall?

Tap to reveal Two codes at every size = 2² = 4× fewer particles at each threshold — typically worth roughly a doubling of hydraulic component life.
Problem 6

Oil report: viscosity +12%, TAN rising, Fe normal, Si normal. Diagnosis and action?

Tap to reveal Oxidation — the fluid is ageing (heat, air, time): viscosity thickening + acid build-up with no wear or dirt signature. Check operating temperature and breather condition, plan an oil change (and address the heat source); FTIR oxidation band confirms. The machine is fine; the lubricant is dying.
Problem 7

ET at 400 kHz on aluminium: how does δ compare to the 100 kHz value of 0.27 mm?

Tap to reveal δ ∝ 1/√f → ×4 frequency = ÷2 depth: δ ≈ 0.13 mm. Sharper surface sensitivity, blinder to depth — frequency is the focus knob.
Problem 8

A gamma source gives 400 µSv/h at 2 m. Dose rate at 10 m, and the lesson?

Tap to reveal Inverse square: ×(2/10)² = ×1/25 → 16 µSv/h. Distance is the cheapest shielding — barriers are set by calculation, then verified by survey meter, never assumed.
Problem 9

You must crack-test an austenitic stainless weld cap. MT is proposed. Comment.

Tap to reveal Rejected: austenitic SS is non-ferromagnetic — MT cannot work. Use PT (fluorescent for sensitivity) for surface-breaking defects, or ET for through-paint scanning; UT/PAUT if subsurface coverage is also required.
Problem 10

Suspected lack of side-wall fusion mid-thickness in a 25 mm butt weld. Pick the method set and justify.

Tap to reveal Planar, subsurface, unfavourably oriented for RT → angle-beam UT/PAUT from both sides (beam normal to the fusion face) with TOFD for height sizing. RT would likely miss it (crack across the beam); PT/MT cannot reach it. Method selection = defect type + orientation + depth, every time.

Key Derivations & Exam Traps

Where R, δ and the 4406 code come from — and the unit traps that sink reports.

Derivation 1 — reflection at an interface
boundary conditions: pressure and particle velocity continuous ⇒ pressure reflection r = (Z₂−Z₁)/(Z₂+Z₁) (amplitude, signed) ⇒ energy reflection R = r² (what tables quote) limits: Z₂ ≫ Z₁ (steel behind water): r → +1, hard mirror Z₂ ≪ Z₁ (air behind steel): r → −1, PHASE-INVERTED mirror

Why it matters: the sign explains why free-surface (back-wall) echoes flip phase while a slag inclusion (denser than a void) may not — a phase clue analysts use to tell crack-like from volumetric reflectors. And the r vs R distinction is a live exam trap: amplitude dB uses 20·log, energy dB uses 10·log.

Derivation 2 — skin depth
Maxwell + Ohm in a conductor → diffusion equation for the field: ∇²B = µσ·∂B/∂t with B = B₀e^(jωt) ⇒ B(x) = B₀·e^(−x/δ)·e^(−jx/δ) δ = √(2/(ω·µ·σ)) = 1/√(π·f·µ·σ) currents at x = δ: 37% amplitude · at 3δ: 5% — effectively gone

Why it matters: the same δ governs eddy-current NDT, induction heating, transformer lamination thickness (→ A32) and why litz wire exists — one derivation, four industries. For ET it also sets the phase-depth relationship: deeper defects lag ~57°/δ, which is exactly how impedance-plane displays size depth.

Derivation 3 — the log₂ logic of ISO 4406
code C for count n (per mL): choose C such that n_upper(C) = 0.01 × 2^C (each code doubles the band) e.g. C=13 → upper 80 · C=14 → 160 · C=18 → 2500 ⇒ ΔC = log₂(n₁/n₂) two codes cleaner = ×4 fewer particles

Why it matters: log coding matches how contamination behaves (multiplicative processes) and how life-extension tables are built — component life gains per code drop are roughly constant, so "two codes cleaner" is a meaningful engineering promise, not lab pedantry.

Seven exam & field traps
  • 1
    The forgotten ÷2. Pulse-echo time is a round trip; d = v·t/2. Doubling a thickness reading destroys a remaining-life calculation.
  • 2
    One velocity per material (and temperature). Steel-calibrated on aluminium reads 7% high; 100 °C shifts steel ~1%. Calibrate, then trend.
  • 3
    20·log vs 10·log. Amplitude ratios use 20·log₁₀, energy uses 10·log₁₀ — mixing them doubles/halves every dB figure in the report.
  • 4
    Orientation blindness. MT misses cracks parallel to flux; RT misses cracks across the beam; UT misses planes parallel to the beam. State the covered orientations in the procedure.
  • 5
    Spectroscopy's size ceiling (~5–8 µm). Calm ppm with rising PQ = large-particle fatigue — the most expensive "green" report in oil analysis.
  • 6
    4406 codes are per mL at >4/>6/>14 µm — quoting "/100 mL" legacy NAS-style numbers against 4406 targets misgrades cleanliness by orders of magnitude.
  • 7
    PT dwell and wash discipline. Short dwell or aggressive washing yields a clean report on a cracked part — process parameters are the sensitivity, log them.
