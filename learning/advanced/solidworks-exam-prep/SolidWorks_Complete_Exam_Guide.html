1The three levels

Everything in the programme sits at one of three levels, and the level tells you what is being measured — recall, competence, or mastery.

ASSOCIATE

CSWA and the -A specialties

For users who have completed a beginner course. Proves fundamental knowledge and basic skills. The entry point for a career or a change of domain.

PROFESSIONAL

CSWP and the -P specialties

For users with a solid foundation who are actively using the software at work — the official guidance is around 40 hours a week for six months or more.

EXPERT

CSWE, CSWE-S

For seasoned professionals. Prerequisites apply, the exams are long and hard, and the retake wait is 90 days rather than 14.

Worth knowing
Dassault report a community of over one million certified users, and the public directory alone lists more than 850,000. Certification is not rare — which is exactly why the combination you hold matters more than any single badge.

3How the testing system works

The client

All testing is delivered through the Tangix TesterPRO Client, downloaded from the 3DEXPERIENCE Certification Center. It runs in its own window, separate from SOLIDWORKS.

  • Windows only. It is not compatible with Macs.
  • An internet connection is required throughout. There is no offline exam.
  • You may run the client on the same machine as SOLIDWORKS (recommended) or on a second machine beside it — but then you need a way to transfer the exam files between them.
  • Dual monitors is the preferred setup: SOLIDWORKS on one screen, the tester on the other.
  • Most licence types are accepted: commercial, research, education, Student Edition, and 3DEXPERIENCE SOLIDWORKS for Makers.

Rules that bite

RuleDetail
No pausingOnce started, an exam cannot be paused. It auto-archives when the clock runs out.
Crash recoveryThe client is designed to resume after a crash or reboot — but the timer does not stop. Security software that wipes the temp folder can defeat recovery entirely.
Retakes14 days between attempts at Associate and Professional level; 90 days at Expert level. No limit on the number of retakes, but a new credit is needed each time.
PrerequisitesNone for any Associate or Professional exam. Expert exams require CSWP plus a number of advanced Professional exams.
Every exam is uniqueCandidates receive individually generated exams, which is why no per-question feedback is ever released.
FeedbackYou get a score and the topic areas to study. Never the questions, never the answers, never the method.
LanguagesAt least 7 on every exam (English, Spanish, Simplified Chinese, French, German, Japanese). Popular exams add Italian, Portuguese, Korean, Traditional Chinese and more.
Extra time (ADA)Available on request with proof of eligibility — email certification@solidworks.com.

After you pass

  • A downloadable PDF certificate and business-card logos from your certification account.
  • A Credly digital badge, issued automatically for Associate, Professional and Expert certifications earned after 1 January 2025. Earlier ones can be generated retroactively from your account.
  • An optional listing in the public certified-user directory. This is off by default — you must switch "List in Online Directory" to Yes in your account settings.
  • Certificates can be validated by anyone via the QR code or certificate ID.
Two minutes that get you found
Turn on the directory listing and claim the Credly badge. A recruiter searching the directory for certified users in Sri Lanka cannot find you if the switch is off — and it is off unless you changed it.

4Getting exams free

There are four legitimate routes to a free exam credit, and most people pay because they do not know about them.

Academic Certification Provider

If your institution is on subscription with enough licences, it can be approved to administer exams and allocate free opportunities to students. Instructors apply through the SOLIDWORKS Education team.

Student licence

Purchasing a student version of SOLIDWORKS comes with a free voucher for the CSWA and the CSWP.

Subscription Service

If your company holds a CAD licence on subscription, free exam vouchers are part of the benefits — along with 400+ on-demand MySolidWorks training videos.

Events and user groups

Certification days at 3DEXPERIENCE World offer free exams to registered attendees, and local SOLIDWORKS User Group (SWUGN) meetings sometimes hand out vouchers.

Bulk purchases can be converted into vouchers and distributed from a VirtualTester account — relevant if your employer ever wants to certify a team.

5CSWA-SD — Sustainability Associate

The odd one out, in three ways: it needs no software at all, it is proctored, and it has the highest pass mark in the whole Associate range.

Length
60 minutes, online proctored
Questions
30 questions, 1 point each
Pass mark
24 of 30 — 80% not the usual 70%
Format
Multiple choice and multi-answer only
Software
None. No SOLIDWORKS licence needed, no models to build
Navigation
Answer in any order; a Summary Screen shows what is unanswered
Retake
14 days
Results
Score and topic areas only — individual question results are not given
Old name
CSDA (Certified Sustainable Design Associate)
The number everyone gets wrong
You need 24 out of 30. That is 80%, and it means you can afford six wrong answers, not nine. Candidates who prepare to a 70% standard are preparing to fail this exam by two marks. There is no partial credit and no weighting — every question is worth exactly one point.

What it is based on

The exam is drawn from the SOLIDWORKS Sustainable Design Guide — ten chapters, roughly three to four hours of reading, and designed to be read in any order. The three question categories are Introduction to Sustainability, Sustainable Design and Environmental Assessment.

The examinable objectives, stated officially

  • Basic concepts of sustainability
  • Sustainable design concepts — Design for Disassembly (DfD), Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) and biomimicry
  • The stages of a product life cycle
  • Sustainability and sustainable business
  • The many faces of sustainable design
  • Setting up an environmental assessment — environmental indicators and scope
  • Goal and scope variables — system boundary and functional unit
  • The basic steps of a Life Cycle Assessment
  • Valuing a product environmental assessment
  • Making theory matter — initial analysis decisions

The six facts that carry the most marks

FactWhy it is tested
Five life cycle stages: raw material → manufacturing → transportation → use → end of lifeThe four-stage list that omits transportation is the single most common wrong answer in the exam
Four indicators: carbon footprint (CO₂e), total energy consumed (MJ), air acidification (SO₂e), water eutrophication (PO₄e)You must know all four with their units. Air gets acid, water gets fed
Functional unit = quantified performance, not the object"One cup" is wrong; "containing 300 ml once" is right. It is what makes two products comparable
ISO 14040 / 14044 and the four LCA phasesGoal & scope → inventory (LCI) → impact assessment (LCIA) → interpretation
Triple bottom line = environment, society, economyDistractors routinely omit the social leg
Xpress vs full SustainabilityXpress is parts only, no use phase, limited materials. Both report the same four indicators
A known teaching conflict — answer from the Guide
The Sustainable Design Guide treats screening-level assessment results as usable for communication and marketing claims. Much of the LCA literature disagrees, because ISO 14044 reserves public comparative assertions for critically reviewed full studies. For the exam, follow the Guide. For professional work, know the caveat. Similarly, remember that transportation must appear in any enumeration of the lifecycle stages — both points were established here from real exam content that contradicted earlier course material.

How to prepare

  1. Read the Sustainable Design Guide end to end once — three to four hours, and it is the actual source text.
  2. Work the CSWA-SD topics in the study app until every topic shows 80% or better. Set your personal target at 90%, because the real pass mark is 80% and you want margin.
  3. Review the SOLIDWORKS Sustainability tutorials, which the official guidance recommends even though no software is required.
  4. Sit the app's mock exam — it mirrors the real format exactly: 30 questions, 60 minutes, 24 to pass.

6CSWA-S — Simulation Associate

Twenty questions, a hundred points, and three of those questions are hands-on FEA problems that can be worth twenty points each.

Pass mark

Format
3 hands-on problems, plus single-answer, multiple-choice, yes/no and multi-select
Point spread
3 to 20 points per question, by difficulty
Software
SOLIDWORKS Premium 2012+, or SOLIDWORKS Simulation 2012+, or Student Edition 2012+
Retake
14 days
Audience
6–9 months of SOLIDWORKS and Simulation experience
The arithmetic that should drive your exam plan
120 minutes ÷ 100 points = 1.2 minutes per point. A 20-point hands-on problem has earned 24 minutes of your time; a 3-point theory question has earned three and a half. Sweep the cheap theory first, then attack the hands-on problems in descending point order. You can lose an entire 20-point problem and still pass with 80.

Assumed background knowledge

The official page states that you are expected to arrive with knowledge in these areas — they are not taught by the exam:

  • Basic view creation
  • Engineering mechanics — statics
  • Strength of materials
  • Finite Element Method / Finite Element Analysis theory

The applied objectives, officially listed

The six things most likely to cost you marks

#TrapThe correct position
1UnitsSI stress defaults to N/m². A legend reading 2.5e8 is 250 MPa. 1 MPa = 1 N/mm² = 10⁶ N/m²
2"Fixed geometry" DOFOn a solid it removes 3 translations (solid nodes have no rotations). On a shell it removes 6
3Default contactGlobal contact is bonded. Parts merely touching behave as one solid unless you define no-penetration
4Force "total" vs "per item"900 N across three faces as total is 300 N each. As per item it is 2,700 N. A factor-of-three error
5SingularitiesIf refining the mesh keeps increasing the peak stress, it is a singularity, not a result. Model the fillet
6Yield strengthNot needed for the stress solve, only for the factor of safety plot. A study can solve and still refuse to give FOS
The 30-second validation that should precede every answer
List the reaction forces. The sum at the fixtures must equal the applied load in the opposite sense. If it does not, stop — do not report the stress. Then check the deformed shape at scale 1, and bound the answer with a hand calculation (σ = F/A, σ = Mc/I, δ = FL³/3EI) to within an order of magnitude.

A far deeper treatment of every one of these topics — solver theory, element formulation, mesh metrics, convergence, hand verification — already exists in your library as A62 CSWA Simulation Exam Guide (19 pages). This section is the exam-facts layer; A62 is the physics.

7CSWP — Design Professional

Three segments, bought and scored separately, taken in any order. Pass one and it never has to be repeated.

Total length
3 hours 20 minutes across all three segments
Software
SOLIDWORKS 2015 or later, plus Microsoft Excel for design tables
Prerequisite
None (K–12 academic candidates must hold CSWA first)
Retake
14 days per segment; only the failed segment is repurchased
Award
CSWP is granted automatically once all three segments are passed
SegmentTimePassRatioSubject
1 — Part modelling from a drawing70 min75 of 10571.4%Equations, linked dimensions, parameter updates, mass properties
2 — Configurations & design tables50 min77 of 10474.0%Deriving configurations, Excel design tables, rearranging features
3 — Assembly modelling80 min77 of 10970.6%Mates, subassemblies, collision, interference, coordinate systems
Three different pass marks
Note that the three segments do not share a pass percentage: 71.4%, 74.0% and 70.6%. Segment 2 is the strictest and the shortest. Do not carry a single "70% will do" assumption into all three.

Segment 1 — what it really tests

You build a part from a drawing whose dimensions are labelled A, B, C, D, E and driven by equations such as X = A/3. Then the exam changes those parameters — repeatedly — and asks for the new mass each time.

The modelling is not the exam. The re-modelling is the exam. A model that rebuilds cleanly when five dimensions change at once passes comfortably. A fragile one fails every update question and runs out of clock. Official topics:

  • Creating a part from a drawing
  • Using linked dimensions and equations to aid modelling; using equations to relate dimensions
  • Updating parameters and dimension sizes
  • Modifying geometry on the initial part to create a more complex part
  • Modifying parameters at different stages while maintaining all other dimensions and design intent
  • Mass property analysis

Segment 2 — configurations and design tables

  • Creating configurations from other configurations (derived configurations)
  • Changing configurations
  • Creating configurations using a Design Table — requires Excel installed
  • Changing or rearranging features of an existing part
  • Mass properties

Design table syntax worth having cold: rows are configurations, columns are parameters. D1@Sketch1 drives a dimension; $STATE@Fillet1 takes S or U for suppression; $COMMENT and $PRP@Description handle comments and properties. Cell A1 is reserved, configuration names run down column A from A3, headers across row 2.

Mass properties report the ACTIVE configuration
Nothing on screen warns you which configuration is active when you open Mass Properties. Reading the mass with the wrong one active is the classic way to lose Segment 2, and the number looks perfectly reasonable.

Segment 3 — assemblies

  • Creating an assembly; adding parts; inserting subassemblies
  • Rigid and flexible subassemblies — a rigid subassembly will not articulate, and gives wrong motion answers silently
  • Replacing a part with another part
  • Basic and advanced mates (Width, Limit, Symmetric, Path, Linear Coupler) and mechanical mates (Gear, Cam, Screw, Slot, Hinge, Rack and Pinion)
  • Collision detection when moving a part — dynamic
  • Interference detection — static, and it reports a volume
  • Creating a coordinate system, and using it to perform mass property analysis
Why coordinate systems and mass properties always appear together
Mass and volume are frame-independent. Centre of mass and moments of inertia are not. The exam pairs the two topics to check you understand that — and reporting the centre of mass in the default frame when a custom coordinate system was asked for gives three plausible, wrong numbers.

8The official CSWP sample exam

Dassault publish a free CSWP practice exam containing five real questions with the answer key. It is drawn from Segment 1 — which the guide itself calls the hardest segment — and it is the closest thing to genuine exam content that exists in public.

Read this before using it
The exam parameters and answer key are reproduced in full below. The drawings are not — the geometry lives in images inside the official PDF, which is free to download from solidworks.com. Model from the images, then use these numbers to check yourself. Do not try to reverse-engineer the geometry from the masses; that turns a rehearsal into a puzzle and teaches you nothing.

Common setup for all five questions

Unit system : MMGS (millimetre, gram, second) Decimal places: 2 Part origin : Arbitrary Material : Alloy Steel, density = 0.0077 g/mm³ (= 7,700 kg/m³) Holes : All holes through all unless shown otherwise F (constant) : Hole Wizard, ANSI Metric Counterbore Type : Hex Bolt — ANSI B18.2.3.5M Size : M8 Fit: Close Through hole Ø15.00 · Counterbore Ø30.00 × 10.00 deep End condition: Through All
Question 1 — Stage 1, initial part · multiple choice

Build the part. A = 213 · B = 200 · C = 170 · D = 130 · E = 41 mm  ·  X = A/3  ·  Y = B/3 + 10 mm

What is the mass of the part in grams?
a) 14139.65   b) 14298.56   c) 15118.41   d) 14207.34

Official answer: d) 14207.34 g
The distractors are built from plausible modelling errors. If your answer is not one of the four, your model is wrong — that is exactly what the options are for. Questions 2, 3 and 5 give you no such safety net.
Question 2 — update parameters · fill in the blank

A = 225 · B = 210 · C = 176 · D = 137 · E = 39 mm  ·  X = A/3  ·  Y = B/3 + 10 mm

Official answer: 16490.45 g
Question 3 — update parameters · fill in the blank

A = 209 · B = 218 · C = 169 · D = 125 · E = 41 mm  ·  X = A/3  ·  Y = B/3 + 10 mm

Official answer: 15100.47 g
B has increased while A and D decreased, and the mass lands between the answers to Q1 and Q2. Checking the direction of change is a cheap way to catch a broken rebuild.
Question 4 — Stage 2, modify · multiple choice

Modify the part per the Stage 2 images; changes are concentrated in areas AA, BB and CC.

A = 221 · B = 211 · C = 165 · D = 121 · E = 37 mm  ·  X = A/3  ·  Y = B/3 + 15 mm

What is the mass of the part in grams?
a) 13095.40   b) 13206.40   c) 13313.35   d) 13395.79

Official answer: b) 13206.40 g
The examinable moment of the whole paper: the equation for Y changed from +10 to +15. A candidate who typed the value as a number now has to hunt for it under time pressure. A candidate who used a global variable edits one character.
Question 5 — Stage 2, update parameters · fill in the blank

A = 229 · B = 217 · C = 163 · D = 119 · E = 34 mm  ·  X = A/3  ·  Y = B/3 + 15 mm

Official answer: 14208.00 g
Note that 14208.00 is only 0.66 g from the Q1 answer of 14207.34, on entirely different geometry and parameters. Coincidences like this are why you must actually rebuild rather than pattern-match.

The three official hints

  • Hint 1 — use linked dimensions (also called shared values) to manage dimensions that must stay equal across stages.
  • Hint 2 — rename your dimensions to A, B, C, D… so you can see at a glance which ones the exam is asking you to change.
  • Hint 3 — as an alternative to Hint 2, drive the changing parameters from a Design Table.

The guide adds two pieces of process advice: save a version of the model after each question, so you can recover and so you can fix errors found later; and do not print the exam — practise switching between the tester window and SOLIDWORKS, because that is what the real thing feels like.

Stated plainly by Dassault
No solutions or methodology will ever be shared by the certification team, for this sample or the real exam. The published disclaimer is blunt: being a professional-level certified user includes the ability to research SOLIDWORKS functionality yourself. The answer key above is all there is.

The CSWE prerequisite, for later
The official CSWP guide states that passing the CSWP plus four of the five specialty (CSWPA) exams is the prerequisite for attempting the CSWE. At $20 per specialty exam, the full expert path is far cheaper than most people assume — the specialties total $80.

10Exam-day checklist

The week before

  • Confirm your exam credit or voucher is in your account
  • Download and install the TesterPRO client, and log in once
  • Confirm Excel is installed and opens (CSWP Segment 2)
  • Confirm your SOLIDWORKS version meets the minimum
  • Sit the mock exam in the study app and clear every topic to target

The hour before

  • Open and warm up SOLIDWORKS before starting the clock
  • Set up dual monitors if you have them
  • Create and note a download folder for exam files
  • Close everything else; disable updates and sleep
  • Check your internet connection is stable — it is required throughout

During

  • Set units and decimal places first, before modelling
  • Apply the exact material named and verify the density
  • Rename dimensions to the exam's letters as you build
  • Save a copy of the model after each question
  • If your answer is not among the options, the model is wrong — go back
  • Use the Summary Screen; answer in any order

After

  • Download your certificate and business-card logo
  • Claim your Credly badge
  • Switch "List in Online Directory" to Yes
  • If you failed: 14 days, new credit, and the results screen names the topics to study

The strategic point
Certifications compound when they tell one story. CSWA + CSWA-AM + CSWP + Sheet Metal + Weldments reads as "this person designs and builds real manufactured hardware". Adding CSWA-S on top says they can also justify it numerically. That is a coherent and rather unusual profile for a mechanical and automation engineer — considerably more valuable than any one badge on its own.
