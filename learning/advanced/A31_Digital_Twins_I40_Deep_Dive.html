What a Digital Twin Really Is

Precise Definition

A digital twin is a fit-for-purpose digital representation of an observable physical element, kept synchronised with that element through a live, two-way data connection — so the model and the asset evolve together in time.

That ISO-aligned wording hides the three words that separate a real twin from a mere 3D model: synchronised, two-way, and fit-for-purpose. A CAD file is a static drawing. A simulation runs offline. A twin is alive — it receives the asset's real state continuously and can push decisions back.

The maturity ladder — not every "twin" is the same

Kritzinger's taxonomy: model = manual both ways · shadow = automatic physical→digital · twin = automatic both ways. The orange return arrow is what makes it a twin.

Product twin

Mirrors one product/serial through its life — design, build, in-service usage, retirement (the "digital thread").

Process twin

Mirrors a production process or line — flow, bottlenecks, OEE, scheduling what-ifs.

System twin

Mirrors a whole plant or fleet — twins of twins, used for strategy and optimisation.

Page 2 · Context

The Four Revolutions — and Where Twins Sit

Industry 4.0 is the fourth industrial revolution: the fusion of physical machines with cyber systems. The digital twin is its signature technology — the place where the "cyber" meets the "physical." This is the full-circle expansion of Micro 33.

1.0 mechanised muscle, 2.0 electrified it, 3.0 automated logic (the PLC — see A07), 4.0 connects it all into a self-aware cyber-physical system.

The 9 pillars of Industry 4.0

IIoT · big-data analytics · the cloud · digital twin / simulation · additive manufacturing (A18) · autonomous robots (A15) · AR · cybersecurity · horizontal/vertical system integration.

CPS — the core idea

A cyber-physical system embeds sensing, computation and networking into physical processes, with feedback loops where the physical affects the digital and vice-versa. A digital twin is the realised, queryable form of a CPS.

Page 3 · The Anatomy

Tao's 5-Dimension Twin Model

The most cited engineering model of a twin (Tao & Zhang) breaks it into five coupled parts. If you can name all five and the connections between them, you understand a twin's architecture completely.

DT = ( PE , VE , Ss , DD , CN ) PE = Physical Entity the real asset + its sensors/actuators VE = Virtual Entity the geometric · physics · behaviour · rule models Ss = Services simulation, prediction, optimisation, diagnostics DD = Digital-twin Data fused physical + virtual + derived knowledge CN = Connections the 6 bidirectional links binding them all

Data (DD) is deliberately at the centre — in a mature twin, the fused data is the asset's truth. Everything else reads from and writes to it.

Page 4 · The Standards

ISO 23247, RAMI 4.0 & the Asset Administration Shell

For twins to interoperate across vendors, three reference frameworks dominate. Examiners love these because they reveal whether you understand architecture, not just hype.

ISO 23247 — Digital Twin Framework for Manufacturing

A four-part standard (overview · reference architecture · digital representation · information exchange). Its functional view stacks three entity layers over the real asset:

OME (a machine, robot, even staff) → device-comm collects & actuates → DT entity represents/simulates → UI entity lets people & services reason and command.

RAMI 4.0

The 3-axis service map

A cube: Layers (asset→business, 6), Life-cycle & value stream (type vs instance, IEC 62890), and Hierarchy (product→connected world, IEC 62264/61512). It tells you where a function lives.

AAS

The standardised twin container

The Asset Administration Shell (IEC 63278-1, governed by the IDTA) is the concrete, file/REST-serialisable digital twin of one asset: submodels for nameplate, documentation, capabilities, condition. ~10 years old in 2025 and now the de-facto I4.0 twin format.

Page 6 · Where Compute Lives

Edge, Fog & Cloud — the Latency Trade

Not all twin computation can sit in the cloud — a safety interlock can't wait 200 ms for a round-trip to a data centre. Industry 4.0 spreads compute across a continuum, placing each task where its latency and data-volume demand.

Rule of thumb: the faster the loop and the bigger the raw stream, the lower in the pyramid it must run. Edge does the reflexes; cloud does the thinking.

Edge

On/next to the machine. Deterministic, real-time filtering, anomaly triggers, the local twin slice. Survives network loss.

Fog

Plant-level gateways/servers. Aggregates many edges, runs line-level models, buffers to cloud.

Cloud

Elastic compute & storage. Fleet-wide ML training, long-horizon simulation, the system twin.

Page 7 · The Transport

MQTT & Sparkplug B — the Twin's Heartbeat

Polling (SCADA's classic model) doesn't scale to a million twin tags. Modern twins use MQTT — a lightweight publish/subscribe broker protocol — so each value is sent only when it changes (report-by-exception) and any number of consumers subscribe without touching the device.

Publishers never know who's listening. Add a new consumer (a twin, an AI, an audit log) and nothing on the device side changes — this is why pub/sub scales.

Sparkplug B — the missing semantics

Raw MQTT is just a pipe; payloads are arbitrary. Sparkplug B (an Eclipse/Cirrus Link spec) standardises the topic namespace, a compact binary payload, and crucially birth/death certificates + state — so a subscriber always knows whether a tag is live, stale, or the device is offline. It turns MQTT into a state-aware backbone fit for a twin.

Topic: spBv1.0/GroupID/NBIRTH/EdgeNodeID spBv1.0/GroupID/DDATA/EdgeNodeID/DeviceID NBIRTH/DBIRTH announce a node; NDEATH (an MQTT "will") fires automatically if it drops.
Page 8 · Inside the Virtual Entity

The Models that Make a Twin Think

The Virtual Entity (VE) is rarely one model — it's a stack. The art is choosing the right modelling style for each question, then fusing them.

Model typeHow it worksBest for
Physics-based (white-box)First-principles equations / FEA / CFD / multibodyStress, thermal, flow — when you need why, not just what
Data-driven (black-box)ML trained on historical sensor dataPatterns too complex to derive — anomaly, RUL prediction
Hybrid (grey-box)Physics + ML correcting its residuals (PINNs)The modern default — accuracy of data, trust of physics
Reduced-order (ROM)A compressed surrogate of a heavy FEA/CFD modelRunning a physics model in real time on the edge
Discrete-eventSimulates queues, flows, states of a processLine throughput, bottleneck & scheduling what-ifs
Predictive maintenance — the killer app

A twin fuses live vibration/temperature with a degradation model to estimate Remaining Useful Life (RUL). It schedules service before failure but after squeezing real life out of the part — neither too early (waste) nor too late (breakdown). Ties straight back to bearings & TPM thinking.

Data assimilation keeps it honest

Models drift from reality. A twin continuously corrects its state with incoming measurements (e.g. a Kalman filter) so the virtual entity tracks the physical one. Without assimilation, a twin slowly becomes a lie.

Page 9 · Active Recall

Interactive — Predictive-Maintenance Twin

A bearing twin tracks vibration. As wear rises, the twin's degradation model lowers the Remaining Useful Life and decides when to raise a work order. Drive the wear and watch the twin reason — the same logic that runs on real rotating equipment.

ISO 10816 zones in miniature: green = good, amber = alert & plan service, red = trip. The twin turns a raw number into a decision.

Page 10 · Security, 5.0 & Recall

Securing the Twin, and the Road to 5.0

A twin is a new, high-value attack surface

Connecting OT to IT is the whole point of Industry 4.0 — and its biggest risk. A twin holds a perfect map of your process and can command it. IEC 62443 is the governing security standard; its defence-in-depth + zones-and-conduits model is non-negotiable for any twin that writes back to the floor.

⚠️ Flat OT/IT network

One breach reaches everything. Segment into zones with firewalled conduits (IEC 62443) and a DMZ between OT and IT.

⚠️ Writable twin, no authz

A twin that can change set-points must authenticate & authorise every command, and log it. Treat write-back as privileged.

⚠️ Model drift unmonitored

An un-assimilated twin diverges silently and people trust its wrong answer. Monitor twin-vs-real residuals as a health metric.

⚠️ "3D model = twin" trap

Selling a static viewer as a twin. No live sync, no two-way link → it's a model. Demand the heartbeat.

Industry 5.0 — the human comes back (full circle to Micro 33)

Where 4.0 optimised for efficiency, Industry 5.0 (the EU's framing) adds three values on top of the same tech stack: human-centricity (cobots augmenting people, not replacing them), sustainability (twins to cut energy & waste), and resilience (twins that war-game disruptions). The twin is the tool that makes all three measurable.

Quick Check

A vendor demos a beautiful rotating 3D CAD model of your pump on a screen and calls it a "digital twin." Using the Kritzinger taxonomy and the ISO definition: (a) what two properties must you verify before accepting that name, and (b) if it has live sensor data flowing in but cannot send anything back, what is it correctly called?

▸ Tap to reveal answer
(a) Verify it is synchronised (a live, automatic data link to the real pump, not a static snapshot) and that the link is two-way / bidirectional (the model can also push decisions or set-points back to the asset). The ISO 23247 wording — "fit-for-purpose representation with synchronization" — makes the live link the test. (b) Data flowing in automatically but nothing flowing back is a digital shadow, not a twin. A static model with no live link at all is just a digital model.
