Page 1 · The Problem

Why plain ± tolerancing quietly fails

Quick Recap → Going Deeper

The micro card said: "GD&T = What it Must Be, Not Just How Big." The MSc reason is that a part has a function, and plain coordinate (±) tolerancing controls size while leaving form, orientation and location ambiguous or wrongly shaped.

Consider a hole located by two ± dimensions, 50 ±0.25 and 30 ±0.25. That defines a square tolerance zone 0.5 mm on a side. But the hole's function — letting a bolt pass — is radially symmetric: it does not care about X and Y separately, only about distance from the true position. The square zone both over-constrains the diagonal direction and under-controls the axial direction. GD&T replaces it with a round zone sized to the actual clearance, recovering area the shop can use.

A round position zone of diameter = diagonal of the square gives 1.57× the area: π(√2·t/2)² ⁄ t² ≈ 1.571. Cheaper parts, identical fit.

Ambiguity

± leaves form & orientation undefined — two inspectors can both "pass" and "fail" the same part.

Wrong shape

Square/rectangular zones don't match round functional requirements.

No origin

± gives no rule for where to measure from — GD&T fixes this with datums.

MSc framing: GD&T is a functional language. Every callout should answer one question — "what does the part have to do, and what geometry guarantees it?" Governed by ASME Y14.5-2018 (USA) and the ISO GPS system (ISO 1101, 5458, 2692, 8015…) internationally.
Page 2 · The Grammar

Reading a Feature Control Frame

Anatomy of the FCF

The Feature Control Frame (FCF) is one "sentence" of GD&T. Read it left-to-right: geometric symbol → tolerance zone shape & value (a leading ⌀ means a cylindrical/round zone) → optional material-condition modifier → up to three datum references in order of precedence (primary, secondary, tertiary), each with its own optional modifier.

Datum order matters: A is contacted first (primary, locks the most DOF), then B, then C. Swapping them changes the measured result.
Two attachment rules: if the leader/frame points at a surface or extension line, the control applies to that surface. If it is placed under a size dimension or aligned with the dimension line / on the diameter, it applies to the feature of size's axis or center plane. This single distinction changes the whole meaning.

Page 4 · The Origin

Datums & immobilizing 6 degrees of freedom

The Datum Reference Frame (DRF)

A datum is a theoretically perfect plane, axis or point derived from a real (imperfect) feature called the datum feature. The three mutually-perpendicular datum planes form the Datum Reference Frame — the part's coordinate origin. Constraining a rigid body means removing all 6 degrees of freedom: 3 translations (X, Y, Z) and 3 rotations (u, v, w).

The classic 3-2-1 location scheme: the primary datum touches at 3 points, the secondary at 2, the tertiary at 1 — a unique, repeatable seating that any inspector or fixture reproduces identically.
Why order is sacred: the primary datum is established first and contacts the high points of the datum feature; the secondary is then made perpendicular to the primary, the tertiary perpendicular to both. Re-order the datums and the part rocks onto different high points → a different coordinate frame → different pass/fail.
Datum targets (Ⓧ symbols with point/line/area targets) handle rough, cast or warped surfaces where full-surface contact is meaningless — defining exactly where the fixture's locators touch.
Page 5 · The Modifiers

MMC · LMC · RFS and bonus tolerance

Material condition modifiers

A feature of size (hole, pin, slot, tab) has a tolerance on its size as well as its geometry. The modifier links the two. Ⓜ MMC = Maximum Material Condition (smallest hole / largest pin — most material). Ⓛ LMC = Least Material Condition (largest hole / smallest pin). RFS = Regardless of Feature Size (the default — no bonus).

Bonus tolerance: every 0.01 mm the hole grows past MMC adds 0.01 mm to the allowable position error — because a bigger hole still passes the same fixed-size pin. Free, functional, and legal.
⌖ ⌀0.10 Ⓜ A B C hole spec ⌀10.00 / 10.20 MMC size = 10.00 → geometric tol = 0.10 Actual hole = 10.14 Bonus = 10.14 − 10.00 = 0.14 Total position tol = 0.10 + 0.14 = 0.24 mm

Use Ⓜ when…

The function is assembly/clearance — bolts through holes. Maximizes yield, enables hard gauges.

Use Ⓛ when…

You must guarantee minimum wall or thread engagement — keep material, no thin spots.

Use RFS when…

Dynamic balance, press fits, sealing — the geometry must hold at every size.

Page 6 · The Workhorse

Position — true position math

Calculating true position error

Position is the most-used location control. The actual feature axis is measured as a deviation (Δx, Δy) from its theoretically exact (basic) dimension — basic dimensions are boxed and carry no tolerance of their own. The radial error is converted to a diameter and compared to the zone.

True position deviation (diametral): TP = 2 · √(Δx² + Δy²) Example: Δx = 0.06, Δy = 0.08 TP = 2·√(0.0036 + 0.0064) = 2·√0.01 = 2·0.10 = 0.20 mm Accept if TP ≤ (geometric tol + bonus).
The radial vector √(Δx²+Δy²) doubles to a diameter so it can be compared directly to the cylindrical zone written in the FCF.
The 57% payoff, quantified: a ±0.25/±0.25 square zone (side 0.5) has area 0.25 mm². Replacing it with a round Position zone whose diameter equals the square's diagonal (⌀0.707) keeps the same worst-case corner limit but gives area π(0.707/2)² = 0.393 mm² — 0.393/0.25 ≈ 1.57, i.e. +57% usable area. Same fit, directly fewer rejects on the same machine.
Page 7 · The Boundary

Virtual condition & datum shift

The constant boundary that guarantees assembly

When Ⓜ is used, GD&T quietly creates a single fixed worst-case boundary — the Virtual Condition (VC). For an external feature (pin): VC = MMC + geometric tol. For an internal feature (hole): VC = MMC − geometric tol. If every part respects its VC, mating is mathematically guaranteed and you can check it with a hard "go" gauge built to the VC size.

Hole ⌀10.00/10.20, ⌖ ⌀0.10 Ⓜ Internal VC = MMC − tol = 10.00 − 0.10 = 9.90 mm Pin ⌀9.80/9.70, ⌖ ⌀0.10 Ⓜ External VC = MMC + tol = 9.80 + 0.10 = 9.90 mm VC(hole) ≥ VC(pin) → 9.90 ≥ 9.90 → assembles ✔

Datum shift is the partner concept: when a datum feature is referenced at Ⓜ, the part can float within the looseness between its actual size and the gauge — an extra location allowance on top of bonus. Both arise from the same idea: real clearance is usable tolerance.

A fixed-limit functional gauge is the physical embodiment of virtual condition — fast, operator-proof attribute inspection at the line.
Page 8 · The Universal Tool

Profile & the ISO GPS future

Profile of a surface — one control to rule them all

Profile ⌓ defines a 3D boundary offset around the true (basic) profile. By choosing the zone distribution it can simultaneously control size, form, orientation and location — which is why model-based-definition (MBD) drawings lean on it heavily. Default zone is bilateral (½ either side); it can be made unilateral or unequally disposed (the Ⓤ symbol with a value).

Profile is the natural language of CAD-driven inspection: the CMM compares scanned points to the nominal model surface and reports deviation against the band.
ASME vs ISO — the philosophy split: ASME Y14.5 follows the Independency Principle by default for surfaces but envelope (Rule #1) for features of size — size limits also bound form. ISO GPS follows Independency by default (ISO 8015), requiring an explicit Ⓔ envelope modifier to couple form to size. Always state which standard governs the drawing.
GPS / MBD direction: ISO Geometrical Product Specification is converging tolerancing, measurement and uncertainty into one chain. With Model-Based Definition the 3D model is the drawing, and Profile + datums carry nearly all the semantics — exactly the skills tested at MSc level.
Page 9 · The Math of Assembly

Tolerance stack-up — 3 methods

Will it always fit? Worst-case vs statistical

A stack-up adds the tolerances along an assembly's dimension chain to predict the gap at the end. Three methods trade safety for cost.

MethodFormula (n contributors)PredictsWhen to use
Worst-Case (WC)T = Σ |tᵢ|100% fit, no rejects everSafety-critical, low volume, few parts
RSS (statistical)T = √(Σ tᵢ²)~99.73% fit (±3σ)High volume, independent, centered processes
Monte Carlosimulate N partsReal yield + shape of gap dist.Skewed/shifted processes, mixed Ⓜ effects
4 plates, each thickness 10 ±0.10 stacked in a 41.00 slot: Nominal stack = 40.00, nominal gap = 1.00 WC : T = 4 × 0.10 = 0.40 → gap = 1.00 ± 0.40 (0.60 … 1.40) RSS : T = √(4 × 0.10²) = 0.20 → gap = 1.00 ± 0.20 (0.80 … 1.20) WC needs 2× the gap budget RSS does — RSS frees tolerance back to the shop.
RSS pitfalls: it assumes contributors are independent, normal and centered. A tool-worn process that drifts toward one limit, or a bimodal supplier mix, breaks the assumption — that is where Monte Carlo (or a Cpk-weighted RSS) earns its keep. Never apply RSS to a 3-part safety stack.
Page 10 · Putting It To Work

Inspection, gauging & a selection workflow

How GD&T is actually verified

CMM

µm

Touch-probe or scanning; software fits datums & reports each FCF. The default for Position/Profile.

Functional gauge

VC

Hard go-gauge at virtual condition — instant attribute check at MMC, no math.

Vision / scanner

3D

Structured-light & CT compare a point cloud to the model — links straight to A16.

Measurement uncertainty is part of the spec: ISO 14253-1 says the measurement uncertainty must be subtracted from the tolerance when deciding conformance — a part near the limit can be rejected because the gauge can't prove it passes. MSc-level tolerancing always budgets the metrology, not just the part.
A repeatable callout workflow
Find the function. What must this feature do — locate, seal, rotate, clear a fastener? The function picks the characteristic.
Choose & order datums. Reference the surfaces that seat the part in assembly, in the order they contact (3-2-1). This is the single biggest source of drawing errors.
Pick the control & zone. Position for clearance holes, Profile for surfaces, Perpendicularity for seats, Runout for rotating parts.
Apply a modifier only if it adds function. Ⓜ for clearance/assembly (and enables hard gauges); RFS for press/seal/balance; Ⓛ to protect minimum wall.
Verify with a stack-up. Prove the chosen tolerances assemble (WC for safety, RSS for volume), then confirm the gauge or CMM routine can measure it within ISO 14253 uncertainty.
Quick Check

A hole is specified ⌀12.00/12.30 with ⌖ ⌀0.20 Ⓜ A B C. A part measures ⌀12.18 with a true-position deviation of TP = 0.34 mm. Pass or fail?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ PASS. MMC = 12.00, so bonus = 12.18 − 12.00 = 0.18 mm. Total allowable position = geometric 0.20 + bonus 0.18 = 0.38 mm. The measured TP of 0.34 ≤ 0.38, so the part conforms — and a functional GO gauge at the internal virtual condition (12.00 − 0.20 = ⌀11.80) would also accept it. This is exactly why Ⓜ recovers yield without risking the fit.
Page 11 · MSc Practice

Worked Examples

Four exam-grade numericals, solved step by step.

Worked Example 1 — Bonus, acceptance and the gauge

Given: hole ⌀10.00–10.40 with ⌖ ⌀0.25 Ⓜ | A | B | C. A part measures ⌀10.32 with true-position deviation 0.50 mm. Accept? What functional gauge verifies this?

MMC = 10.00 bonus = actual − MMC = 10.32 − 10.00 = 0.32 allowable TP = 0.25 + 0.32 = 0.57 mm measured 0.50 ≤ 0.57 → ACCEPT virtual condition (internal) = MMC − geo tol = 10.00 − 0.25 = ⌀9.75 gauge: GO pin ⌀9.75 at true position, datum-seated on A|B|C

Discussion: the part failed the "0.25 on the drawing" reading but passes the standard — because Ⓜ promises only that the ⌀9.75 boundary stays clear, and a larger hole can wander further while keeping it clear. The gauge checks that boundary directly: no arithmetic, no CMM, one insertion. This is the economic heart of MMC: yield recovered with zero functional risk.

Worked Example 2 — Floating vs fixed fastener sizing

Given: two plates joined by M6 bolts (max ⌀6.0) through clearance holes ⌀6.6 min. Size the position tolerances for (a) both plates with clearance holes (floating), (b) one plate tapped (fixed).

floating fastener: T = H − F = 6.6 − 6.0 = ⌀0.6 for EACH plate fixed fastener: T₁ + T₂ = H − F = 0.6 even split: 0.3 / 0.3 · common split: 0.4 (clearance) / 0.2 (tapped) tapped-hole tolerance carried in a projected tolerance zone Ⓟ extending the zone through the mating plate's thickness

Discussion: floating gets the full clearance per part because each bolt can drift with its own pair of holes; fixed shares one budget because the stud dictates from the tapped side. The Ⓟ modifier is not decoration — a tilted tapped hole passes an in-plate check yet the protruding stud misses the clearance hole; projecting the zone measures the tilt where it actually bites.

Worked Example 3 — Stack-up: worst case vs RSS

Given: four spacers 25.0 ± 0.1 stack inside a housing bore 100.6 ± 0.15. Find the minimum end gap worst-case, and statistically (RSS, tolerances ≈ 3σ).

nominal gap = 100.6 − 4×25.0 = 0.6 mm worst case: gap_min = (100.6−0.15) − (100.0+4×0.1) = 100.45 − 100.4 = 0.05 mm RSS: T_gap = √(4×0.1² + 0.15²) = √0.0625 = 0.25 mm gap = 0.6 ± 0.25 → min ≈ 0.35 mm at the 3σ level (~99.7%)

Discussion: worst case guarantees assembly at ±0.05 breathing room; RSS says the realistic 3σ minimum is 0.35 — seven times more comfortable — because four independent ±0.1 errors almost never align. Design rule: WC for safety-critical or low-volume; RSS for high-volume with process control (and Monte Carlo when distributions are non-normal or contributions nonlinear). Quote which method the drawing note assumes.

Worked Example 4 — CMM result to true position

Given: CMM reports a hole axis at dx = +0.12, dy = −0.09 from true position; hole measures ⌀12.18; callout ⌖ ⌀0.20 Ⓜ, MMC ⌀12.00. Conformance?

radial offset r = √(0.12² + 0.09²) = √0.0225 = 0.15 mm TP (diametral) = 2r = 0.30 mm ← the ×2 is mandatory bonus = 12.18 − 12.00 = 0.18 allowable = 0.20 + 0.18 = 0.38 ≥ 0.30 → ACCEPT

Discussion: position is a cylindrical zone diameter, so the radial deviation is doubled — forgetting the ×2 is the most common CMM-report misreading in industry (it flips accept/reject decisions in both directions). Note the workflow is identical to Example 1: geometry → bonus → boundary. One method, every position callout.

Problem Set — 10 Questions

Work each one before revealing — the FCF grammar and the boundary algebra answer everything.

Problem 1

CMM: dx = 0.05, dy = 0.12. True position?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: TP = 2√(0.05²+0.12²) = 2×0.13 = ⌀0.26 mm. Radial 0.13, diametral 0.26 — always report which.
Problem 2

Hole ⌀12.00–12.30, ⌖ ⌀0.20 Ⓛ A B C. Part at ⌀12.05 — allowable TP?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: LMC = 12.30; bonus = LMC − actual = 12.30 − 12.05 = 0.25; allowable = 0.20 + 0.25 = 0.45 mm. Ⓛ pays bonus as the feature departs from LMC — used to protect minimum wall/edge distance, mirror-image of Ⓜ.
Problem 3

Pin ⌀7.8–8.0 with ⊥ ⌀0.10 Ⓜ A. Virtual condition, and what does the mating hole need?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: External feature: VC = MMC + geo = 8.0 + 0.10 = ⌀8.10. The mating hole's own virtual condition must be ≥ 8.10 — i.e. hole MMC minus its geometric tolerance ≥ 8.10. Fits are designed boundary-to-boundary, not size-to-size.
Problem 4

M8 bolts (max ⌀8.0), both plates clearance-holed at ⌀8.9 min. Position tolerance per plate?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: Floating fastener: T = H − F = 8.9 − 8.0 = ⌀0.9 each plate (at MMC, with bonus growing from there).
Problem 5

Flatness, parallelism, perpendicularity: which need a datum, and what does each control?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: Flatness — form only, NO datum, zone floats with the surface. Parallelism / perpendicularity — orientation, datum required, zone locked to 0°/90° from the datum but free in location. All three are two-parallel-plane zones; only the anchoring differs. (Orientation controls also cap form as a by-product.)
Problem 6

Under ASME Rule #1, a shaft ⌀10 ± 0.2 measures ⌀9.8 everywhere. How much can it bow?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: Rule #1: perfect form at MMC — the ⌀10.2 envelope may not be violated. At ⌀9.8 the form error may consume the full departure: bow up to 0.4 mm. ISO defaults to independency (ISO 8015) — size and form separate unless Ⓔ is added: the classic trans-Atlantic drawing trap.
Problem 7

Five dimensions each ±0.1 stack linearly. Worst-case vs RSS total?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: WC = 5×0.1 = ±0.5; RSS = √5 × 0.1 ≈ ±0.224. The √n saving is why statistical tolerancing exists — and why it must be backed by process control (each ±0.1 really ≈ 3σ) before you spend it.
Problem 8

Why does datum ORDER (|A|B|C| vs |B|A|C|) change measurement results on the same part?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: The order is the 3-2-1 seating sequence: primary takes 3 points of contact (kills 3 DOF), secondary 2, tertiary 1. Swap the order and the part physically seats differently in the DRF — every measured coordinate shifts. The correct order is the ASSEMBLY contact order, which is why datum selection is a functional decision, not a drafting one.
Problem 9

A shaft seal surface needs its rotation error controlled. Circular runout vs total runout — choose and justify.

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: Total runout — it sweeps the full surface (all circular elements simultaneously) about the datum axis, catching taper and cylindricity error as well as eccentricity, which a lip seal cares about along its whole contact band. Circular runout checks one section at a time — adequate for a groove, not for a seal land. Both are measured as TIR on rotation.
Problem 10

Tolerance zone 0.20; CMM expanded uncertainty U = 0.02. Per ISO 14253-1, what is the guaranteed-conformance zone?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: U is subtracted at BOTH limits: conformance zone = 0.20 − 2×0.02 = 0.16. Parts measuring in the two 0.02 guard bands are neither provably good nor provably bad — the metrology eats 20% of this tolerance, which is why gauges are chosen with U ≤ ~10% of the zone (the 10:1 rule).

Key Derivations & Exam Traps

The algebra behind the boundaries — and the misreadings that scrap good parts.

Derivation 1 — why TP = 2√(dx²+dy²), and the 57% gain
the tolerance zone is a CYLINDER of diameter T about true position axis offset r = √(dx²+dy²) conforms iff r ≤ T/2 ⇔ 2r ≤ T vs the old ±t square zone: the circle circumscribing the square has diameter √2·(2t) — a round zone of that size accepts every part the square did PLUS the corners: area gain = (π(√2t)²)/(4t²) − 1 ≈ 57%

Why it matters: the ×2 convention and the 57% argument are the twin justifications you must produce in any design review that still tolerances holes with ±. Function is radial; the zone should be too.

Derivation 2 — the constant boundary (bonus & VC are one fact)
internal feature at Ⓜ: worst intrusion into the assembly space = (actual size) − (allowed TP)/1 … minimised when smallest & most displaced VC = MMC − T_geo (constant, size-independent) conformance ⇔ surface stays outside VC ⇔ TP_allowed(actual) = T_geo + (actual − MMC) ← "bonus" is just algebra

Why it matters: bonus tolerance is not a gift or a loophole — it is the SAME acceptance criterion re-expressed per part size. Understanding this collapses MMC, bonus, virtual condition and functional gauging into one sentence: keep the fixed boundary clear. (At Ⓛ, mirror everything: VC = LMC + T_geo, protecting material minimum instead of clearance.)

Derivation 3 — RSS from variance addition
independent dimensions: Var(ΣXᵢ) = Σ Var(Xᵢ) σ_gap = √(Σσᵢ²) ; with each tolerance tᵢ ≈ 3σᵢ: T_RSS = √(Σ tᵢ²) (same 3σ confidence as the inputs) correlated or non-normal contributors → Monte Carlo, not RSS

Why it matters: RSS is legitimate exactly as far as its assumptions: independent processes, centred and capable (Cpk ≥ 1 → A27 Six Sigma). A shifted process (the 1.5σ shift!) or a common fixture error correlates the terms and quietly re-creates the worst case you thought you had engineered away.

Seven exam & field traps
The missing ×2. CMM radial deviation vs diametral TP — the single most common cause of wrong accept/reject calls.
Ⓜ on a datum feature ≠ bonus. It grants datum shift — the whole pattern may translate as the datum departs from MMC; individual zones do not grow.
ASME Rule #1 vs ISO 8015. ASME: perfect form at MMC by default. ISO: independency by default (add Ⓔ for envelope). State the governing standard in the title block — parts legal in one system fail the other.
Profile zones are bilateral by default. A 0.5 profile zone is ±0.25 about true profile unless unilaterally disposed (U modifier / unequally-disposed note).
Position needs a ⌀ to be cylindrical. Omit it and the zone is two parallel planes per direction — a different (and usually unintended) requirement.
Tapped holes without Ⓟ. In-plate verification passes, the protruding stud still misses — always project the zone through the mating thickness.
Datum features are imperfect too. Measurements simulate datums with gauges/fitting algorithms (max-inscribed, minimax); two labs fitting differently can disagree on the same part — specify the fitting method for tight work.

Hands-on ladder — eight exercises
Read ten drawings. Pull ten real FCFs from your plant's drawings and translate each into one plain-English sentence (zone, shape, anchor, modifier).
Bonus tables. For one Ⓜ hole callout, tabulate allowable TP for five actual sizes MMC→LMC — the bonus line becomes muscle memory.
Boundary sketch. Draw the VC boundaries for a mating pin+hole pair and prove the fit clearance boundary-to-boundary.
Fastener drill. Size position tolerances for a 4-bolt floating joint and an 8-bolt fixed joint from the fastener/hole specs alone.
Stack one gap. Build the WC and RSS chain for a real assembly gap in a spreadsheet; watch which contributor dominates and re-budget it.
Seat a part. On a surface plate with gauge pins and a height gauge, physically 3-2-1 a bracket and feel datum order change the numbers.
Shadow a CMM run. Watch (or write) the datum-fitting sequence for one part program; find where the report says radial vs diametral.
Re-tolerance a bad drawing. Take one ±-everything legacy drawing and convert its critical features to functional GD&T — then defend it in a design review.
Self-assessment — can you…?

◔ Recall

Write the FCF grammar, TP = 2√(dx²+dy²), VC = MMC ∓ geo, and the floating/fixed fastener formulas from memory.

◑ Apply

Compute bonus and accept/reject any Ⓜ/Ⓛ callout from a CMM report, and size a functional gauge.

◕ Analyse

Build a WC and RSS stack for an assembly gap, defend which method applies, and identify the dominant contributor.

● Design

Choose datums from assembly function, pick characteristic + modifier per feature, and survive both an ASME and an ISO audit of the same drawing.
