⚡Relays & Contactors

Tiny coil controls huge current — the electromechanical switch.

Quick Definition

A relay uses a small control current to magnetically switch a large load current. A contactor is the same idea — but bigger, built for motor and high-current loads.

Try It — Energize the Coil
Coil: OFF · Contacts: OPEN · Lamp: OFF
Relay vs Contactor

📍 Relay

  • Switches: up to ~10 A
  • Loads: signal, lamps, fans
  • Size: finger-tip to matchbox
  • Sound: tiny click
  • Examples: PLC output relay, automotive

🏗️ Contactor

  • Switches: 10 A to 1000+ A
  • Loads: motors, heaters
  • Size: fist to shoebox
  • Sound: loud thunk
  • Examples: motor starter, DOL panel
Key Facts
  • ✓ Provides galvanic isolation — control ⟂ power
  • ✓ NO (Normally Open) / NC (Normally Closed) contacts
  • ✓ Coil voltages: 24 V DC, 110 V, 230 V AC common
  • ✓ Contactors paired with overload relays for motor protection
  • ✓ SSR (Solid-State Relay) = same job, no moving parts
Real Industrial Example
🏭

A conveyor motor is switched by a contactor (handles 15 A at 400 V), and the contactor's coil is energized by a small relay output on the PLC (typical 24 V DC) — perfect separation of weak signal and strong load.

Quick Check

Q: Why is a relay better than connecting a PLC output directly to a 5 kW motor?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: A PLC output sources at most a few hundred mA at low voltage — it would burn instantly carrying a motor's amps. The relay/contactor lets the PLC's tiny signal command a heavy load safely, with full electrical isolation between the two circuits.
