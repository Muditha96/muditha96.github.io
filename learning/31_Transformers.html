🔄Transformers

Step up, step down, isolate — magnetic coupling, no wires touching.

Quick Definition

A transformer uses two coils sharing a magnetic core to change AC voltage up or down. No electrical connection between sides — perfect isolation, ratio set by turns count.

4 Common Transformer Types
⬇
Step-Down

High voltage in, low voltage out. Grid 11 kV → 400 V.

⬆
Step-Up

Low voltage in, high voltage out. Generator → grid transmission.

🛡️
Isolation (1:1)

Same voltage, no electrical connection. Safety on lab supplies.

📊
Current Transformer

Measures big AC currents — ring around a busbar.

Key Facts
  • ✓ V₁/V₂ = N₁/N₂ = I₂/I₁ (power conservation)
  • ✓ Works only on AC — needs changing flux to induce voltage
  • ✓ Galvanic isolation = safety, no DC path between sides
  • ✓ Efficiency typically 95–99%; losses = copper + iron + eddy
  • ✓ Three-phase: Dyn11 wye-delta is the workhorse
Real Industrial Example
⚡

A factory substation takes 11 kV from the grid and steps it down to 400 V (line) / 230 V (phase) — feeding panels, motors, lighting. One transformer handles MW-scale power with no moving parts.

Quick Check

Q: Why does the grid transmit at 400,000 V instead of just 230 V from the power station?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: Power = V × I. For the same power, higher V means lower I → much less I²R loss in the cables. Step up to 400 kV for long-distance transmission, step down at substations for distribution. Without transformers, the grid wouldn't exist.
