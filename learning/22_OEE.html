📈OEE

Overall Equipment Effectiveness — manufacturing's single most important KPI.

Quick Definition

OEE measures what % of planned production time is truly productive — combining 3 losses into one score: Availability × Performance × Quality.

OEE = A × P × Q

The 6 Big Losses (Source of Low OEE)
A · Breakdowns

Unplanned stops — motor failure, broken sensor, jam.

A · Changeovers

Setup time when switching product or recipe.

P · Minor Stops

Brief jams, blocked sensors, restarts < 5 min.

P · Reduced Speed

Machine runs slower than design rate.

Q · Startup Rejects

Bad parts produced while warming up / dialing in.

Q · Production Rejects

Scrap and rework during steady-state running.

Key Facts
  • ✓ World-class OEE = 85% (A 90 × P 95 × Q 99)
  • ✓ Average factory OEE worldwide: ~60%
  • ✓ Multiplication is brutal — three 90% factors = 73% OEE
  • ✓ SCADA & MES systems calculate OEE automatically
  • ✓ Pair with TPM (Lesson coming) to systematically improve it
Real Industrial Example
🏭

A pharma packaging line shows OEE 62% on the SCADA. Root-cause: 22% time lost to changeovers between products. SMED training cuts changeover from 40 → 12 min → OEE jumps to 78%.

Quick Check

Q: A line ran 7 hr of 8 planned, made 90% of target output, and had 2% scrap. What's the OEE?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: A = 7/8 = 87.5%. P = 90%. Q = 98%. OEE = 0.875 × 0.90 × 0.98 = 77.2%. Decent, but ~8 points below world-class.
