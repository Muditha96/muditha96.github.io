⚙️Gears & Ratios

Trade speed for torque (or vice versa) — the engineer's lever.

Quick Definition

A gear pair transmits motion and force between shafts. By choosing tooth counts, you trade speed for torque — without losing power (in theory).

4 Common Gear Types
⚙️
Spur

Parallel shafts, straight teeth. Simple, noisy, cheap.

🌀
Helical

Angled teeth — smoother & quieter, but creates axial thrust.

📐
Bevel

Shafts at 90°. Found in differentials & drill heads.

🪱
Worm

Huge ratio (50:1+), self-locking. Slow + very strong.

Key Facts
  • ✓ Ratio = T_driven ÷ T_driver
  • ✓ Output speed = Input speed ÷ ratio
  • ✓ Output torque = Input torque × ratio × efficiency
  • ✓ Module (m) = pitch diameter ÷ tooth count (mm)
  • ✓ Backlash = play between teeth — minimised in servo gearboxes
Real Industrial Example
🛗

An industrial elevator uses a worm gearbox with a 50:1 ratio — a 1500 RPM motor turns the drum at 30 RPM with 50× the torque. If power fails, the worm self-locks the load in place.

Quick Check

Q: Your motor delivers 5 Nm at 3000 RPM. After a 1:10 gear reduction, what comes out?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: Speed = 3000 ÷ 10 = 300 RPM. Torque = 5 × 10 = 50 Nm (ignoring losses). The gearbox is a torque amplifier — paid for in speed.
