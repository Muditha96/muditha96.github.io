🛢️Hydraulics

Massive force through pressurized oil — the muscle of heavy machinery.

Quick Definition

Hydraulics uses incompressible oil under high pressure (typically 100–350 bar) to deliver enormous force for presses, lifts, and heavy machines — with precise position control.

Pascal's Law — Force Multiplication
10 N ↓
Area: 1 cm²
100 N ↑
Area: 10 cm²
Same pressure on both pistons → bigger piston = bigger force.
F₂ = F₁ × (A₂ / A₁) → 10 N × 10 = 100 N
Pneumatics vs Hydraulics

Pneumatics (Air)

  • 🌬️ 6 bar typical
  • ⚡ Fast, springy
  • 🧼 Clean, safe
  • 💪 Small-medium force
  • 💰 Cheaper

Hydraulics (Oil)

  • 🔥 100–350 bar
  • 🎯 Stiff, precise
  • ⚠️ Leaks, heat, noise
  • 🏋️ Massive force
  • 💎 Premium cost
Key Facts
  • ✓ Oil is ~incompressible → stiff & precise positioning
  • ✓ Pressure × area = force (Pascal's Law)
  • ✓ Components: pump, reservoir, valve, cylinder, cooler
  • ✓ Force density 50× higher than pneumatics
  • ✓ Top brands: Bosch Rexroth, Parker, Eaton, Danfoss
Real Industrial Example
🏗️

A 2000-ton stamping press forms car body panels using hydraulics — generating 20 million newtons of force from a single ram, repeated 300 times per hour.

Quick Check

Q: Why is hydraulics preferred over pneumatics for a 200-ton injection-moulding press?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: Air is compressible — at 6 bar you'd need an impossibly large cylinder. Oil at 200 bar gives 30× more force per area, holds position rigidly, and lets you fine-tune speed under load.
