📊SCADA & HMI

The window into every factory — see, monitor, and control.

Quick Definition

HMI = Human-Machine Interface (single machine touchscreen). SCADA = Supervisory Control & Data Acquisition (plant-wide system that monitors many machines & logs data).

HMI vs SCADA

📟 HMI

  • Scope: One machine
  • Location: On the floor
  • Users: Operators
  • Logs: Limited / recent
  • Examples: Siemens TP, AB PanelView

🖥️ SCADA

  • Scope: Whole plant
  • Location: Control room / cloud
  • Users: Supervisors, engineers
  • Logs: Historian — years
  • Examples: Ignition, WinCC, FactoryTalk
Key Facts
  • ✓ Reads tags from PLCs via OPC-UA, Modbus, EtherNet/IP
  • ✓ Trends, alarms, recipes, reports, user roles
  • ✓ Historian stores time-series data for OEE & analytics
  • ✓ Web/mobile clients let you monitor from anywhere
  • ✓ Top platforms: Ignition, WinCC, AVEVA, FactoryTalk
Real Industrial Example
🥤

A beverage plant runs SCADA across 6 bottling lines — one screen shows live OEE, alarms, and tank levels. Engineers analyze 6 months of trend data to find the bottleneck.

Quick Check

Q: A small machine builder wants to display 4 setpoints and 6 readings. HMI or SCADA?

Tap to reveal answer ↓ A: HMI — single machine, on-floor interface. SCADA would be overkill. Use SCADA only when you need plant-wide visibility, historical trending, or multiple operators.
